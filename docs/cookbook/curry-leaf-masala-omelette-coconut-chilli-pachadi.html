Curry-Leaf Masala Omelette with Coconut-Chilli Pachadiకరివేపాకు మసాలా ఆమ్లెట్, కొబ్బరి పచ్చిమిర్చి పచ్చడి

A thick, fluffy omelette flecked with finely chopped curry leaves, ginger and green chilli, served with a thick coconut and green-chilli pachadi that has a crackling mustard-and-curry-leaf tempering on top. Breakfast with real Andhra attitude in 22 minutes.

Method
  1. Make the pachadi first. Blitz the coconut, green chillies, garlic, ginger, cumin, lemon juice, 1 g salt and 3 to 4 tablespoons of water for 20 to 30 seconds into a thick, slightly coarse chutney; it should hold a spoon, not pour. Scrape into a small bowl.
  2. Temper it: heat the coconut oil in a small pan over medium. Add the mustard seeds, and when they crackle (about 15 seconds) add the dried chilli and whole curry leaves for 10 seconds, until the leaves stop spluttering. Tip it over the pachadi, without stirring, so the tempering sits on top.
  3. Beat the eggs with the cream, turmeric, pepper, karam and 2.5 g salt for about 30 seconds, until evenly yellow with no streaks. Mix the chopped onion, green chillies, ginger, sliced curry leaves and coriander together in a small bowl and divide into two portions.
  4. Heat half the ghee (7 g) in the nonstick pan over medium. Add one portion of the masala and fry for 40 to 60 seconds, until the onion turns translucent and the curry leaves smell toasty. This keeps the omelette from going watery and gives it a cooked-masala flavour.
  5. Pour in half the egg mix (3 eggs) and swirl to cover the pan. Reduce to medium-low, cover, and cook 2 minutes until the base is golden and the top is just set but still glossy. Fold in half and slide onto a plate.
  6. Wipe the pan, add the remaining ghee and masala, and repeat for the second omelette. Serve each with half the pachadi on the side. Eat hot, dipping each wedge of omelette into the pachadi.
Make it taste like home
  • Flavour: slice the curry leaves very fine (stack and cut across) so every bite has some. Cooking the masala in the ghee first gives a sweeter, rounder flavour than raw onion folded into the eggs.
  • Keto: use unsweetened coconut. Fresh or thawed frozen grated coconut (Indian grocery freezer aisle) gives the right creamy texture, and a small onion measure keeps the omelette near 6 g net carbs. The traditional chana dal and tamarind in a kobbari pachadi are left out; lemon juice supplies the tang.
  • Make ahead: the pachadi (without tempering) keeps 2 days chilled; re-temper before serving. The omelette is best eaten hot, within minutes.
Swaps
  • Lower heat (to about 2): omit the green chilli and karam from the omelette and use 1 green chilli in the pachadi.
  • Dairy-free: replace the cream with a spoon of water and the ghee with coconut oil.
