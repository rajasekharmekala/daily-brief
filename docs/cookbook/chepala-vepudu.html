Chepala Vepudu (Andhra Fish Fry with Karam Masala)చేపల వేపుడు

Fish pieces marinated in ginger, garlic and lemon, then crusted in a freshly ground roasted sesame, dry coconut and red chilli masala and shallow-fried in ghee with curry leaves. Crisp edges, fiery and ready in about 25 minutes.

  • 5.8 g net carbs
  • 37 g protein
  • 18 g fat
  • 338 kcal
  • 24 min
  • Serves 2
Fat 49%Protein 44%Net carbs 7%per serving
Ingredients
  • tilapia fillets, cut into 5 cm pieces2 large fillets, about 12 oz · 350 g
  • lemon juice2 tsp · 10 g
  • ginger, grated2 cm piece · 6 g
  • garlic, grated3 cloves · 8 g
  • Guntur red chilli powder, for the marinade1 1/2 tsp · 3 g
  • turmeric1/2 tsp · 1 g
  • black pepper1/4 tsp · 0.5 g
  • salt3/4 tsp · 3.5 g
  • sesame seeds, for the masala2 tsp · 6 g
  • dry coconut, unsweetened desiccated, for the masala1 tbsp · 8 g
  • cumin seeds, for the masala1 tsp · 2 g
  • dried red chillies, for the masala3 chillies · 3 g
  • ghee1 tbsp · 14 g
  • sesame (gingelly) oil1/2 tbsp · 7 g
  • curry leaves2 sprigs · 3 g
  • onion, thin rings, to servea few rings · 40 g
Method
  1. Make the karam vepudu masala (4 min): dry-roast the sesame, dry coconut, cumin and dried chillies in a small pan over medium-low heat, stirring, until the sesame pops and the coconut turns pale gold. Cool 1 minute and grind to a coarse, sandy powder with a pinch of salt. Set aside.
  2. While the masala cools, pat the fish very dry. Mix the ginger, garlic, lemon juice, 1 1/2 tsp chilli powder, turmeric, pepper and the salt into a thick paste and rub it on the fish. Rest 10 minutes, no longer; the lemon starts to cure the surface.
  3. Press each fish piece into the masala so it is lightly coated on both sides. Keep a spoonful aside.
  4. Heat the ghee and sesame oil in a flat pan over medium-high heat until shimmering. Lay in the fish in a single layer without crowding (fry in two batches if needed) and leave undisturbed 3 minutes, until the underside is crusted and golden.
  5. Flip gently with a thin spatula, add the curry leaves to the oil and fry 2 to 3 minutes more. The fish is done when it flakes easily with a fork and reaches 145 F (63 C).
  6. Sprinkle the reserved masala over the fish for the last 30 seconds, then serve at once with onion rings, lemon and a bowl of cauliflower rice.
Make it taste like home
  • Fish choices at US stores: tilapia fillets and US-farmed catfish are cheap and always available; thick salmon fillet gives a richer fry; frozen whole or steaked pomfret, kingfish (surmai/vanjaram) and rohu or katla from Indian grocers are closest to home. Fillets and steaks both work; whole fish needs 4 to 5 minutes a side.
  • Taste tip: the masala is roasted, not raw; that smell is the difference between a good fish fry and a great one. It keeps 2 weeks in a jar, so make double.
  • Keto tip: the traditional chana and urad dal in this masala are dropped (the carbs add up); sesame and coconut give the same nutty texture at about 6 g net carbs per serving including the onion rings.
  • Do not turn the fish early; it sticks until the crust forms and then releases easily.
Swaps
  • Catfish or pomfret instead of tilapia; add 1 to 2 minutes for thicker steaks.
  • For heat 4, use two dried chillies in the masala and 1 tsp chilli powder in the marinade.
