Dinner Heat Guntur

Paneer Bhurji, Guntur Style (Curry Leaf and Karam)పనీర్ బుర్జీ

Crumbled paneer in a ghee tempering of mustard, cumin and crisp curry leaves, finished with a spoon of cream and a hit of Guntur karam. It is the quick, juicy, south-Indian cousin of the dhaba bhurji, and it is on the table in 22 minutes.

  • 9.1 g net carbs
  • 22 g protein
  • 38 g fat
  • 462 kcal
  • 22 min
  • Serves 2
Fat 73%Protein 19%Net carbs 8%per serving
Ingredients
  • paneer, crumbled by handabout 7 oz block · 200 g
  • ghee1.5 tbsp · 18 g
  • mustard seeds1/2 tsp · 1.5 g
  • cumin seeds1/3 tsp · 1 g
  • dried red chilli2 small · 2 g
  • fresh curry leaves2 to 3 sprigs · 6 g
  • garlic, finely chopped1 large or 2 small cloves · 4 g
  • ginger, finely choppeda 2 cm piece · 4 g
  • onion, finely choppedabout 2 tbsp · 25 g
  • green chillies, finely chopped2 to 3 · 10 g
  • tomato, finely chopped2 tbsp · 30 g
  • turmeric powder1/3 tsp · 1 g
  • Guntur red chilli powder (karam)1 heaped tsp · 3 g
  • coriander (dhaniya) powder1 tsp · 2 g
  • heavy cream4 tsp · 20 g
  • coriander leaves, choppeda small handful · 8 g
  • lemon juice1 tsp · 5 g
  • saltscant 1/2 tsp · 2 g
Method
  1. Crumble the paneer coarsely with your hands: pea-sized crumbs with a few bigger chunks, not a fine grate, so it stays juicy. Chop the onion, tomato, chillies, ginger and garlic. Measure the spices into one small bowl.
  2. Heat the ghee in a kadai over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds and wait about 20 seconds until they crackle, then add the cumin and dried chillies for 10 seconds.
  3. Add the curry leaves (they spit, so tilt a lid in front of you) and fry 20 to 30 seconds until crisp and glassy. Lift out about a third onto a plate with a spoon; these are your crunchy garnish.
  4. Add the garlic, ginger and green chillies and stir 40 seconds until the garlic is pale gold. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook 2 minutes, splashing in a spoon of water if it catches, until soft and light gold. Add the tomato and cook 2 minutes until mushy and the ghee glistens at the edges.
  5. Lower the heat. Add the turmeric, Guntur karam and coriander powder with 2 tablespoons of water and stir 20 seconds until the raw smell of the powders disappears and the masala looks like a thick, red paste. Stir in the cream.
  6. Add the paneer and the remaining salt. Toss gently for 2 to 3 minutes just until the paneer is hot and coated in masala; it should look moist, not dry. Longer than this makes it rubbery.
  7. Off the heat, add the lemon juice and coriander leaves, scatter the fried curry leaves over the top, and serve straight from the pan.
Make it taste like home
  • Flavour: the ghee does the heavy lifting here. Do not swap for oil, and add the cream only after the spices have cooked, so the masala fries first and then goes silky.
  • Keto: the paneer carries about 4 g net carbs per serving on the Amul label (the label's sugar figure; homemade paneer usually reads lower), so the onion and tomato are kept to a measured 25 g and 30 g for the whole pan. Eat it with a spoon, or wrapped in a lettuce leaf or a crisp cabbage leaf.
  • Make ahead: the cooked masala (to step 5) keeps 2 days chilled. Reheat, add crumbled paneer and finish. Leftover bhurji keeps 1 day and reheats with a splash of water.
Swaps
  • Lower heat (to about 2): deseed the chillies and use 1.5 g (half a teaspoon) of karam.
  • Without cream: use 1 tbsp of whole milk or a spoon of full-fat Greek yogurt stirred in off the heat.
