Gongura Pachadi (Roselle Leaf Pickle, No Jaggery)గోంగూర పచ్చడి

The sour, fiery gongura pachadi you spoon onto hot cauliflower rice with ghee: leaves wilted in sesame oil, ground with roasted chilli, coriander and fenugreek, then tempered. Sorrel is sour enough that it needs neither tamarind nor jaggery.

  • 2.8 g net carbs
  • 1 g protein
  • 6 g fat
  • 72 kcal
  • 40 min
  • Serves 8
Fat 77%Protein 7%Net carbs 16%per serving
Ingredients
  • gongura (roselle) leaves, picked, washed and fully driedabout 8 cups packed · 300 g
  • sesame oil, divided3 tbsp · 40 g
  • dried red chillies, divided: 6 for the powder, 2 for the tempering8 chillies · 12 g
  • coriander seeds1 tbsp · 8 g
  • fenugreek seeds¼ tsp · 1 g
  • cumin seeds1 tsp · 3 g
  • garlic cloves4 cloves · 8 g
  • mustard seeds1 tsp · 2 g
  • urad dal for the tempering1 tsp · 3 g
  • curry leaves1 sprig · 3 g
  • salt1 tsp · 6 g
Method
  1. Pick the gongura leaves off the stems, wash them well and spread them on a clean cotton towel. Leave until bone dry, at least 1 to 2 hours in a breezy spot (longer is better). Wet leaves spit in the oil and spoil the pickle.
  2. Dry-roast 6 of the chillies with the coriander, fenugreek and cumin in a kadai on medium for 2 to 3 minutes, until the chillies darken a shade and the spices smell toasty. Cool, then grind with the garlic and half the salt to a fine powder.
  3. Heat 2 tbsp of the oil in the kadai on medium-high. Add the leaves in handfuls and stir 5 to 6 minutes until they collapse into a dark olive mush, all the moisture is gone and the raw sour smell mellows. Cool 5 minutes, then crush with a ladle or pulse once or twice. Traditional pachadi stays coarse.
  4. Mix the spice powder into the leaves.
  5. Heat the remaining oil in the same kadai. Add the mustard seeds and wait for them to crackle, then the urad dal until golden (30 to 40 seconds), then the 2 remaining chillies, broken, and the curry leaves until crisp. Stir in the gongura mixture with the remaining salt and cook on low 3 to 4 minutes until the oil floats on the surface and the pachadi pulls away from the sides. Taste for salt and chilli.
  6. Cool completely before spooning into a clean, dry glass jar.
Make it taste like home
  • Storage: refrigerate in a clean dry glass jar with a layer of oil on top and a dry spoon. Swasthi's oil-rich version keeps 3 to 4 weeks; Archana's lighter-oil version is best within about a week.
  • Keto note: gongura leaves count about 4 g net carbs per 100 g. There is no jaggery, tamarind, onion or tomato, and the dal is 3 g, which makes a 2 tbsp serving only a few grams of net carbs.
  • Where to shop: fresh or frozen gongura at Indian grocers; thaw frozen leaves and squeeze them very dry before frying.
  • Eat it over cauliflower rice with ghee, or alongside egg curry or a dry chicken fry.
Swaps
  • Lower heat: use 5 chillies in total.
  • More oil keeps the pickle longer; less oil makes it lighter but best eaten within a week.
