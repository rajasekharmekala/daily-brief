Kanava Vepudu (Andhra-Style Pepper Squid Fry)కనవా వేపుడు

Squid rings tossed over fierce heat with garlic, ginger, curry leaves, crushed pepper and toasted coconut flakes so they stay springy and wear a dry, spicy crust. Cooked in under 10 minutes once the pan is hot.

  • 9.5 g net carbs
  • 23 g protein
  • 12 g fat
  • 247 kcal
  • 20 min
  • Serves 2
Fat 45%Protein 39%Net carbs 16%per serving
Ingredients
  • cleaned squid, tubes cut into 1 cm rings (tentacles kept)about 10 oz · 280 g
  • coconut oil1 tbsp · 14 g
  • mustard seeds1/2 tsp · 1 g
  • dried red chillies, as Guntur karam2 chillies plus a pinch of powder · 2.5 g
  • curry leaves1 to 2 sprigs · 2 g
  • garlic, minced3 cloves · 8 g
  • ginger, minced2 cm piece · 8 g
  • green chilli, slit2 chillies · 8 g
  • onion, finely sliced2 tbsp · 25 g
  • black pepper, coarsely crushed1 tsp · 2 g
  • turmeric1/4 tsp · 1 g
  • unsweetened desiccated coconut1 1/2 tbsp · 8 g
  • lemon juice1 tbsp · 15 g
  • saltabout 1/2 tsp · 2.5 g
Method
  1. Pat the squid very dry with paper towels, then toss with turmeric, half the pepper and the salt. Dry squid fries instead of stewing.
  2. Heat the coconut oil in a kadai on high until it shimmers. Add mustard seeds; when they pop, add dried chillies and curry leaves and fry 20 seconds until the leaves stop spluttering.
  3. Add garlic, ginger, green chilli and onion; fry 2 minutes on medium-high until the onion softens and the garlic just turns gold.
  4. Turn the heat to high, add the squid in one layer and let it sear 1 minute undisturbed. Stir and fry 2 to 3 minutes only: squid turns opaque and curls quickly, and longer cooking makes it rubbery. Any water it releases should evaporate within about 2 minutes.
  5. Add the coconut and remaining pepper and toss 1 minute until the coconut smells toasted and the masala clings dry.
  6. Finish with lemon juice and serve immediately.
Make it taste like home
  • Taste tip: stay close to the pan; squid is done when opaque and just firm, no more than 4 to 5 minutes in total.
  • Keto: squid carries about 3 g carb per 100 g, which is counted; no tamarind used (the Kerala-style sources add a little).
  • Storage: eat fresh. If keeping leftovers, refrigerate within 2 hours and use within 2 days; reheat quickly to steaming hot (165 F / 74 C), and expect firmer squid.
Swaps
  • Mild: use one green chilli and omit the dried chillies.
  • Frozen cleaned squid rings work: thaw, then pat very dry.
