Dinner Heat Coastal Andhra

Kodi Kobbari Kura (Coconut Chicken Curry)కోడి కొబ్బరి కూర

Coastal Andhra comfort: roasted coconut ground coarse with coriander, cumin and dried chilli, stirred into onion-curry-leaf chicken and finished with a splash of coconut milk. Rich, nutty, thick and just spicy enough.

  • 8.5 g net carbs
  • 43 g protein
  • 31 g fat
  • 498 kcal
  • 30 min
  • Serves 2
Fat 58%Protein 35%Net carbs 7%per serving
Ingredients
  • boneless skinless chicken thigh, cut into 3 cm pieces (weekday swap for bone-in)about 3 to 4 thighs · 400 g
  • turmeric powder1/4 tsp · 1 g
  • salt3/4 tsp · 3 g
  • ginger, crushed1 inch piece · 8 g
  • garlic, crushed3 cloves · 8 g
  • whole dried red chillies3 chillies · 3 g
  • coriander seeds1 tbsp · 6 g
  • cumin seeds1 tsp · 2 g
  • grated coconut, unsweetened (frozen is fine, thawed)2/3 cup, packed · 50 g
  • coconut oil1 tbsp · 14 g
  • mustard seeds1/3 tsp · 1 g
  • fresh curry leavesabout 1 1/2 sprigs · 4 g
  • onion, finely choppedabout 1/2 small onion · 50 g
  • green chillies, slit2 · 10 g
  • canned unsweetened coconut milk4 tbsp · 60 g
  • cinnamon, cloves and cardamom (garam masala pinch)a pinch each, ground · 1 g
  • lemon juice1 tsp · 5 g
  • coriander leaves, choppeda small handful · 5 g
Method
  1. Cut the thighs into 3 cm pieces and toss with the turmeric, salt, and half of the ginger and garlic. Set aside while you roast the spices.
  2. Dry roast the dried chillies, coriander and cumin in a dry kadai over medium-low heat for 1 minute until fragrant. Add the grated coconut and roast 2 to 3 minutes more, stirring constantly, until it is pale golden and smells nutty. Cool 3 minutes, then grind with 50 ml water to a coarse, slightly grainy paste (not smooth).
  3. Heat the coconut oil in the same kadai over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds; when they crackle, add the curry leaves and onion with a pinch of salt and fry 4 to 5 minutes until golden. Add the slit green chillies and the remaining ginger and garlic and fry 1 minute.
  4. Add the chicken and sear on high heat for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring now and then, until it turns opaque on the outside. Stir in the coconut masala and 100 ml water, scraping up anything stuck to the base.
  5. Cover and simmer on medium-low for 10 minutes, stirring once, until the chicken is just cooked through (74 C / 165 F). Pour in the coconut milk and the garam masala pinch, and cook uncovered for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring, until the gravy is thick, glossy and clinging (this is a dry-ish kura, not a soupy curry).
  6. Finish with the lemon juice and coriander leaves. Rest 2 minutes and serve.
Make it taste like home
  • Taste tip: roast the coconut until it is pale gold, not brown: that gives the nutty sweetness a jaggery-free curry needs. Darker tastes burnt and bitter.
  • Keto tip: canned coconut milk varies by brand; check the label for no added sugar and use a full-fat one. 60 g adds only about 1.7 g net carb for the whole pot. Fresh grated coconut is the main carb here (50 g is about 3 g net), so do not double it.
  • Make-ahead: the roasted coconut-spice paste (without water) keeps 3 days in the fridge; reheat the curry with a splash of water since the coconut thickens as it stands.
  • Weekday note: boneless thigh in 3 cm pieces. Bone-in curry cut needs 25 minutes covered instead of 10.
Swaps
  • Heat 4 (your usual): use 4 dried chillies and add 1 tsp Guntur chilli powder to the marinade.
  • Skip the coconut milk and finish with a tablespoon of ghee for a drier, vepudu-style kura.
