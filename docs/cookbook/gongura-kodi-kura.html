Gongura Kodi (Sorrel Leaf Chicken Curry)గోంగూర కోడి కూర

Gongura is the sour soul of Andhra cooking: wilted into a thick, tangy masala until it melts, then clung to tender thigh pieces with a roast-and-ground coriander, fennel and fenugreek powder. No tamarind needed, because the leaf is the sourness.

  • 42 g protein
  • 20 g fat
  • 396 kcal
  • 30 min
  • Serves 2

Ingredients
  • boneless skinless chicken thigh, cut into 2.5 cm pieces (weekday swap for bone-in)about 3 to 4 thighs · 400 g
  • gongura (sorrel) leaves, stems removed, chopped (thawed and squeezed if frozen)about 2 loosely packed cups · 100 g
  • coriander seeds2 tsp · 4 g
  • fennel seeds1/3 tsp · 1 g
  • fenugreek seedsa pinch · 0.5 g
  • whole dried red chillies2 chillies · 2 g
  • sesame oil (nuvvula noone)1 1/2 tbsp · 20 g
  • mustard seeds1/3 tsp · 1 g
  • cumin seeds1/2 tsp · 1 g
  • fresh curry leavesabout 1 1/2 sprigs · 4 g
  • onion, finely choppedabout 1/2 small onion · 40 g
  • garlic, crushed3 cloves · 8 g
  • ginger, crushed3/4 inch piece · 6 g
  • green chillies, slit2 · 10 g
  • tomato, finely choppedabout 1/3 medium tomato · 40 g
  • Guntur red chilli powder1 1/2 tsp · 4 g
  • turmeric powder1/4 tsp · 1 g
  • cinnamon and cloves, ground (0.5 g cinnamon + 0.3 g cloves)a pinch of each · 0.8 g
  • salt3/4 tsp · 3 g
Method
  1. Pick the gongura leaves off the stems, wash and pat them dry, then chop roughly. (Frozen leaves from the Indian grocer: thaw and squeeze out the water.) Cut the thighs into 2.5 cm pieces.
  2. Dry roast the coriander, fennel, fenugreek and dried chillies in your kadai over medium-low heat for 90 seconds until fragrant. Cool for 2 minutes and grind with the cloves and cinnamon to a coarse powder. Do not roast the fenugreek longer than that or it turns bitter.
  3. Heat the sesame oil in the same kadai over medium-high. Add the mustard seeds; when they crackle, add the cumin and curry leaves and let them splutter for 10 seconds. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and fry 4 to 5 minutes until golden at the edges.
  4. Add the ginger, garlic and slit green chillies and fry 1 minute until the raw garlic smell is gone. Stir in the chilli powder and turmeric, then the tomato, and cook 2 to 3 minutes, mashing it down with the back of the spoon, until it is soft and the oil begins to separate.
  5. Add the gongura and the remaining salt. It collapses almost instantly; keep stirring on medium-high for 3 to 4 minutes until the leaves melt into a dark, thick paste and the oil shows at the edges. This is where the sourness mellows.
  6. Add the chicken and the ground masala, stir to coat, then pour in 80 ml of water. Cover and simmer on medium-low for 10 minutes, stirring once, until the chicken is cooked through (74 C / 165 F). Uncover, raise the heat and cook 2 to 3 minutes until the gravy is thick and clinging. Taste for salt and serve hot.
Make it taste like home
  • Taste tip: red-stemmed gongura is the sourer one; green-stemmed is milder. If your leaves are very sour, add the tomato first and a squeeze more ghee at the end; do not reach for jaggery.
  • Keto tip: gongura is about 4 g net carb per 100 g, so 100 g for two keeps the leaf generous but counted. Skip the onion boost many recipes use and keep to 40 g.
  • Where to buy: fresh gongura shows up at Indian grocers in summer; frozen leaves (often labelled sorrel or ambadi) are in the freezer section year round. Spinach plus 1 tsp lemon is the emergency swap but not the same dish.
  • Make-ahead: the gongura masala base (steps 3 to 5) keeps 4 days in the fridge; add chicken and simmer when you are ready. The curry tastes better on day two.
  • Weekday note: boneless thigh in 2.5 cm pieces, covered for 10 minutes. Bone-in gongura mamsam is a 45-minute weekend dish.
Swaps
  • Heat 3: drop the green chillies and halve the chilli powder.
  • Bone-in weekend version: use bone-in thigh pieces and simmer covered for 25 minutes in step 6.
