Menthi Mamsam Keema (Goat Mince with Fresh Fenugreek)మెంతికూర మటన్ కీమా

Lean goat mince browned hard with ginger, garlic and curry leaves, then finished with a big handful of fresh menthi kura whose pleasant bitterness cuts through the ghee and heat. Dry, deeply savoury and sharply spiced, a lunch that eats like a weekend dish in 30 minutes.

  • 8.9 g net carbs
  • 40 g protein
  • 18 g fat
  • 370 kcal
  • 30 min
  • Serves 2
Fat 45%Protein 45%Net carbs 10%per serving
Ingredients
  • goat (mutton) mince, from an Indian or halal butcherabout 12 oz · 350 g
  • ghee1 tbsp · 14 g
  • groundnut (peanut) oil2 tsp · 10 g
  • cinnamon, ground1/2 tsp · 1 g
  • green cardamom pods, crushed3 pods · 1 g
  • cumin seeds1/2 tsp · 1 g
  • curry leaves2 sprigs · 3 g
  • onion, finely chopped1/4 medium onion · 40 g
  • ginger, minced2 cm piece · 6 g
  • garlic, minced3 cloves · 8 g
  • green chillies, slit2 chillies · 8 g
  • Guntur red chilli powder2 1/4 tsp · 4.5 g
  • turmeric1/2 tsp · 1 g
  • coriander (dhaniya) powder1 1/2 tsp · 3 g
  • plain whole-milk yogurt, whisked2 tbsp · 30 g
  • fresh fenugreek (menthi) leaves, picked from thick stems, washed and choppedabout 2 packed cups · 100 g
  • garam masala1 tsp · 2 g
  • lemon juice1 tsp · 5 g
  • coriander leaves, chopped1 tbsp · 5 g
  • salt1/2 tsp · 3 g
Method
  1. Prep (7 min): wash the fenugreek in two changes of water, pick the leaves and chop roughly. Rinse the mince in a strainer if it is from a counter, and drain well.
  2. Heat the ghee and oil in the kadai over medium-high heat. Add the cinnamon, cardamom and cumin, then the curry leaves; wait 20 seconds until the curry leaves stop spluttering.
  3. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and fry 4 minutes until golden at the edges. Add ginger, garlic and green chillies and fry 1 minute until the raw smell goes.
  4. Add the mince and break it up with the back of the spoon. Fry on high heat for 5 to 6 minutes, stirring often, until the moisture cooks off and the meat browns and starts to stick; this browning is the flavour.
  5. Lower the heat to medium and add turmeric, chilli powder, coriander powder and salt; stir for 30 seconds, then the yogurt and stir until the oil begins to separate (about 1 minute). Add 1/4 cup water, cover and cook on low for 8 to 10 minutes until the goat is tender. (Pressure-cooker shortcut: 1 whistle on high, then 5 minutes on low.)
  6. Add the fenugreek leaves, stir, and cook uncovered on medium heat for 4 to 5 minutes until they wilt, lose their raw bitterness and the keema is dry and glossy. Raw fenugreek is bitter, so do not cut this step short.
  7. Finish with garam masala, lemon juice and coriander leaves, and rest 2 minutes before serving.
Make it taste like home
  • Taste tip: browning the mince hard before adding any water is what gives a deep, roasted flavour without tomato or tamarind.
  • Keto tip: no tomato, tamarind or sugar here; the yogurt and lemon give the tang and the whole dish is about 9 g net carbs per serving, mostly from onion, garlic and fenugreek.
  • Heat: drop the chilli powder to 2 tsp (about 4 g) and use one green chilli for heat 3.
  • Make-ahead: keeps 3 days in the fridge and freezes for up to 3 months; the flavour deepens overnight.
Swaps
  • Fresh fenugreek not available: use half the weight of frozen menthi, or soak 2 tbsp kasuri methi in water for 15 minutes and squeeze it out.
  • Lamb or chicken mince also work; chicken needs only 10 minutes covered.
