Guntur Karam Paneer Pepper Fry

Golden paneer gets a fast, dry toss with cracked pepper, Guntur chilli, curry leaves, and lemon. It works as a savory tiffin plate when you want a break from an egg-based breakfast.

8.7g net carbs  ·  25g protein  ·  34g fat  ·  435 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 69% ● Protein 23% ● Net Carbs 8%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
Paneer Pepper Fry ASMR — Chef Shrey
Ingredients
  • full-fat paneer, 2 cm cubes about 1 1/2 cups · 240g
  • onion, thinly sliced 35g
  • tomato, finely chopped 30g
  • green chilli, slit 1 small · 8g
  • ginger, finely grated 6g
  • garlic, finely chopped 6g
  • fresh curry leaves 3g
  • groundnut oil 10g
  • black pepper, freshly cracked 2g
  • Guntur chilli powder 2g
  • coriander powder 2g
  • turmeric 1g
  • salt 2g
  • cilantro, chopped 5g
  • lemon juice 8g
Method
  1. Pat the paneer dry. If it is very firm, soak the cubes in warm water for 5 minutes, then drain well; this helps them stay tender while they brown.
  2. Heat a wide skillet over medium-high heat. Add half the oil and arrange the paneer in one layer. Leave it undisturbed for 2 minutes until the undersides turn golden, then turn and brown another side. Transfer to a plate.
  3. Lower the heat to medium and add the remaining oil. Add the curry leaves and green chilli; when the leaves stop spluttering, stir in the onion and cook until the edges turn amber, about 3 minutes.
  4. Add ginger and garlic and stir for 30 seconds. Add tomato, turmeric, coriander powder, and chilli powder; cook until the tomato softens and the masala looks glossy, 2 to 3 minutes. If the pan looks dry, add a tablespoon of water.
  5. Add the paneer and cracked pepper. Toss gently for 1 to 2 minutes so the spices cling without breaking the cubes. Turn off the heat, finish with cilantro and lemon, and serve hot.
  6. For less heat, remove the green-chilli seeds and use 1 g chilli powder; keep the black pepper for its warmer aroma.
🌉 US & Bay Area Ingredient Adaptations
  • Paneer: Extra-firm tofu from Trader Joe's or Safeway works if paneer is unavailable; press it dry first and brown it gently.
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers in Santa Clara or New India Bazaar in Sunnyvale carry paneer; frozen paneer is also common at Indian grocers.
  • Guntur chilli powder and curry leaves: Use plain ground cayenne in a smaller measured amount for Guntur chilli; frozen curry leaves can replace fresh leaves.
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers (Santa Clara), New India Bazaar (Sunnyvale), and other South Bay Indian grocers.
Make it taste like home
  • Crack pepper just before cooking; fine pre-ground pepper loses the little bursts of aroma that define this fry.
  • Add the chilli powder after the tomato goes in so it blooms in moisture instead of scorching in hot oil.
  • Serve immediately for browned edges; leftovers reheat best in a skillet over low heat with a teaspoon of water.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • Use sesame oil for a nuttier Andhra-style finish.
  • If using salted packaged paneer, reduce the added salt and taste at the end.

Andhra Kodi Iguru with Browned Onion Masala

Chicken cooks in its own juices under a lid, then reduces into a thick, clingy masala with curry-leaf and freshly ground spice. A squeeze of lemon sharpens the finish without needing a rice-side sauce.

6.3g net carbs  ·  43g protein  ·  16g fat  ·  355 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 42% ● Protein 51% ● Net Carbs 7%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
Onion Chicken | ఉల్లిపాయ చికెన్ మసాలా | ఉల్లిపాయలతో ఇలా కర్రీ ట్రై చేయండి. కారం కారం గా భలే ఉంటుంది — Vismai Food
Ingredients
  • boneless skinless chicken thighs, cut into 3 cm pieces 420g
  • onion, finely sliced 35g
  • tomato, chopped 35g
  • ginger, finely chopped 10g
  • garlic, finely chopped 10g
  • green chillies, slit 2 small · 10g
  • curry leaves 3g
  • mustard seeds 2g
  • cumin seeds 2g
  • coriander powder 4g
  • Guntur chilli powder 3g
  • turmeric 1g
  • ground black pepper 1g
  • groundnut oil 12g
  • salt 3g
  • cilantro, chopped 8g
  • lemon juice 8g
Method
  1. Pat the chicken dry and toss with the salt, turmeric, chilli powder, and half the coriander powder. While it rests for 5 minutes, coarsely grind the cumin and black pepper.
  2. Heat the oil in a heavy kadai over medium heat. Add mustard seeds; when they crackle, add curry leaves and green chilli. Stir for 10 seconds, then add onion.
  3. Cook the onion, stirring often, until it turns deep gold at the edges, about 5 minutes. Add ginger and garlic and cook until their raw smell fades, about 45 seconds.
  4. Add the ground cumin-pepper and remaining coriander powder. Stir for 20 seconds, then add tomato and cook until it collapses into a thick masala and the oil shows at the rim.
  5. Add chicken and turn each piece to coat. Cover and cook over medium-low heat for 12 minutes; the chicken will release liquid, so do not add water. Uncover and cook 4 to 6 minutes, stirring gently, until the gravy clings and the thickest piece reaches 165°F (74°C).
  6. Turn off the heat, rest for 3 minutes, then finish with cilantro and lemon. For lower heat, use one green chilli and 1 g chilli powder.
🌉 US & Bay Area Ingredient Adaptations
  • Boneless chicken thighs: Boneless chicken breast from Costco, Safeway, or Trader Joe's can be used; cut it evenly and begin checking a few minutes earlier so it stays moist.
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Fresh or frozen chicken thighs are easy to find at Costco, Safeway, Trader Joe's, and South Bay Indian grocers.
  • Guntur chilli powder and fresh curry leaves: Use measured cayenne or Kashmiri chilli powder for the heat/color balance; frozen curry leaves work if fresh are unavailable.
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers in Santa Clara and New India Bazaar in Sunnyvale stock Indian chilli powders and curry leaves.
Make it taste like home
  • Cut the chicken to an even size so it finishes during the short covered simmer.
  • The masala is ready for the chicken when the tomato loses its raw smell and a fine sheen of oil appears around the edge.
  • The thick iguru reheats well with a spoonful of water over low heat; avoid boiling it hard after reheating.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • Use sesame oil instead of groundnut oil for a stronger Andhra aroma.
  • Add a small handful of chopped cilantro stems with the ginger and garlic for a greener, sharper masala.

Gongura Chepa Kura (Sorrel Fish Curry)

Tender fish sits in a bold, sour-green gongura masala with Guntur chilli and curry leaves. Cooking the sorrel first lets its sharpness settle into the curry rather than tasting raw or harsh.

9.2g net carbs  ·  46g protein  ·  12g fat  ·  327 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 32% ● Protein 56% ● Net Carbs 11%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
GonguraChepalaPulusu|Sorrel Fish Masala|గోంగూరా ఈ మసాలాలు తో పులుసు పెడితే మళ్ళీ ఇదే కావాలంటారు — Vismai Food
Ingredients
  • tilapia fillets, cut into large pieces 420g
  • gongura leaves, washed and thick stems removed 150g
  • onion, finely chopped 35g
  • tomato, chopped 35g
  • green chillies, slit 8g
  • ginger, finely chopped 8g
  • garlic, finely chopped 8g
  • curry leaves 3g
  • cumin seeds 2g
  • mustard seeds 2g
  • Guntur chilli powder 3g
  • coriander powder 3g
  • turmeric 1g
  • tamarind pulp 3g
  • sesame oil 12g
  • salt 3g
  • cilantro, chopped 8g
Method
  1. Pat the tilapia dry and season lightly with a pinch of the salt and turmeric. Heat half the sesame oil in a wide skillet over medium-high heat; sear the fish for about 45 seconds per side, just until it firms. Lift onto a plate.
  2. Lower the heat to medium. Add the remaining oil, then cumin and mustard seeds. When they crackle, add curry leaves and green chilli; stir until the leaves stop spluttering.
  3. Add onion and cook until translucent with light golden edges, about 3 minutes. Stir in ginger and garlic for 30 seconds, then add tomato, chilli powder, coriander powder, remaining turmeric, and salt. Cook until the tomato softens and the masala turns glossy.
  4. Add gongura, cover for 2 minutes to wilt, then uncover and stir for 5 to 7 minutes until the leaves darken and lose their raw grassy smell. Mash the leaves into the masala with the spoon or pulse briefly for a smoother gravy.
  5. Stir in the measured tamarind and 120 g water. Simmer for 3 minutes, taste the sourness, and add the seared fish in one layer. Cover and simmer gently for 5 to 7 minutes, until the thickest piece reaches 145°F (63°C) or flakes with light pressure.
  6. Turn off the heat and let the curry stand for 5 minutes so the fish takes on the gongura gravy. Finish with cilantro. If using red-stem gongura, taste before adding tamarind because it is often more sour.
🌉 US & Bay Area Ingredient Adaptations
  • Tilapia and gongura: Tilapia from Costco or Safeway is a practical stand-in for freshwater fish; frozen chopped gongura works after thawing and squeezing out excess water.
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers in Santa Clara or New India Bazaar in Sunnyvale often carry frozen gongura; 99 Ranch and mainstream supermarkets carry tilapia.
  • Fresh gongura: If gongura is unavailable, use French sorrel or a mix of baby spinach and a little lemon juice; taste before adding the recipe's small amount of tamarind.
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Check Indian grocers such as Patel Brothers and New India Bazaar for frozen leaves; Whole Foods or farmers markets may have sorrel seasonally.
Make it taste like home
  • Sear the fish only briefly before it enters the gravy; the slow simmer finishes cooking it without breaking the fillets.
  • Keep the curry at a quiet simmer after adding fish. A hard boil can make delicate tilapia fall apart.
  • Serve with a small portion of cauliflower rice or extra sautéed greens; the curry is already tangy and does not need a sweetener.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • Use catfish or another firm white fish instead of tilapia.
  • For a milder bowl, remove the seeds from the green chillies and halve the chilli powder.

Nutrition Journal: Oct 22 · 5m
A UK Biobank protein analysis finds different cardiovascular associations for vegetable- and animal-based low-carb patterns  —  Using 19,655 UK Biobank participants and a median 11.6 years of follow-up, researchers compared plasma-protein signatures linked to low-carbohydrate diet scores based on 24-hour dietary recalls. The total-pattern signature was not associated with cardiovascular disease (hazard ratio 0.98, 95% CI 0.84–1.14); the vegetable-based signature was inversely associated (0.77, 0.66–0.91), while the animal-based signature was not (0.96, 0.83–1.12). This observational analysis is not a keto trial and cannot establish cause and effect; confirmation in independent populations is still needed.
Takeaway: For your keto-science reading, it is a reminder that evidence on broad low-carbohydrate patterns does not automatically describe a ketogenic menu.
