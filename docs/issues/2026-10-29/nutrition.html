Muttakose Guddu Vepudu (Cabbage and Egg Stir-fry)ముట్టకోస గుడ్డు వేపుడు

Shredded cabbage steams down in a mustard, cumin and curry-leaf talimpu until sweet at the edges, then soft Guntur-spiced eggs are scrambled right through it. A one-pan, 20-minute breakfast that eats like a proper vepudu, not a salad.

6.2g net carbs  ·  15g protein  ·  22g fat  ·  285 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 70% ● Protein 21% ● Net Carbs 9%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
Best Cabbage Egg Bhurji Recipe — Vismai Food
Ingredients
  • cabbage, finely shredded about 1 3/4 loosely packed cups · 130g
  • large eggs 4 eggs · 200g
  • ghee 1 tbsp · 14g
  • groundnut oil 1 tsp · 5g
  • mustard seeds 1/2 tsp · 1.5g
  • cumin seeds 1/2 tsp · 1g
  • whole dried red chillies, broken 2 chillies · 2g
  • fresh curry leaves about 15 leaves · 3g
  • garlic, finely chopped 1 large clove · 4g
  • onion, finely sliced 1 heaped tbsp · 12g
  • green chillies, slit 2 chillies · 10g
  • Guntur red chilli powder 1 tsp · 3g
  • turmeric powder 1/4 tsp · 1g
  • freshly grated or frozen coconut 2 tsp · 10g
  • coriander leaves, chopped a small handful · 5g
  • salt to taste · 3g
Method
  1. Shred the cabbage as finely as you can with a knife or the thin blade of a box grater; fine shreds wilt in minutes. Crack the eggs into a bowl with a pinch of salt and a pinch of the chilli powder and beat lightly, just until the whites and yolks combine.
  2. Heat the ghee and groundnut oil in the kadai over medium-high heat. Add the mustard seeds; when they crackle and pop (about 20 seconds) add the cumin and broken red chillies and fry for 10 seconds until the cumin darkens a shade and smells toasty.
  3. Add the curry leaves (stand back, they spit) and the garlic and fry for about 30 seconds until the garlic smells sweet and just starts to colour, then add the onion and green chillies and cook for 1 to 2 minutes until the onion goes translucent.
  4. Stir in the turmeric and the rest of the chilli powder for 10 seconds, then tip in the cabbage and salt. Toss well to coat in the masala, cover and cook on medium-low for 4 to 5 minutes, stirring once or twice. Do not add water; the cabbage cooks in its own steam. Uncover and cook 1 to 2 minutes more until any liquid is gone and the edges just catch colour.
  5. Push the cabbage to one side, pour the beaten eggs into the cleared space and let them set for 20 seconds, then scramble gently and fold the cabbage back through. Cook 1 to 2 minutes until the eggs are just set and glossy; they continue cooking off the heat.
  6. Fold in the grated coconut and coriander, check salt and serve straight away, with a squeeze of lemon if you like.
🌉 US & Bay Area Ingredient Adaptations
  • Fresh curry leaves: Frozen curry leaves (no need to thaw); skip rather than use dried, which have almost no flavour
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers (Santa Clara), New India Bazaar (Sunnyvale); frozen packs at most Indian grocers
  • Guntur red chilli powder: Any hot Indian red chilli powder; or 1/2 tsp cayenne plus 1/2 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder for colour
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers or New India Bazaar; cayenne in any Safeway or Trader Joe's
  • Freshly grated coconut: Frozen grated coconut (thawed), or 1 tbsp unsweetened desiccated coconut soaked 5 minutes in hot water and squeezed
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Frozen aisle at Patel Brothers or 99 Ranch; unsweetened desiccated coconut at Whole Foods or Sprouts
  • Cabbage: Any green cabbage, or a bag of pre-shredded coleslaw mix without dressing (a little carrot adds about 1 g carb)
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Any supermarket; Costco and Trader Joe's sell shredded cabbage bags
Make it taste like home
  • Shred the cabbage fine and keep the heat moderate so it sweetens and steams instead of going soggy; letting the last of the moisture cook off is what makes this a vepudu.
  • The eggs go in last and soft: pull the pan off the heat while they still look slightly wet.
  • Keto count: cabbage is counted in grams here (130 g shreds); a heaped extra cup adds roughly 3 g net carbs, so weigh it.
  • Make-ahead: shred the cabbage and slice the onion the night before; the talimpu is 2 minutes of work.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • For a gentler 2 to 3 heat, drop the green chillies and use 1/2 tsp chilli powder.
  • Dairy-free: use sesame oil or groundnut oil in place of the ghee.

Kodi Keema Beans Vepudu (Chicken Mince and Green Bean Stir-fry)కోడి కీమా బీన్స్ వేపుడు

A dry, spicy keema fry in the Andhra style: minced chicken thigh browned in sesame oil with onion, ginger, garlic and green chilli, then tossed with finely cut beans and a dusting of coconut. It is the kind of dry fry that disappears into a lunchbox, and it carries more flavour than its short ingredient list suggests.

9.1g net carbs  ·  43g protein  ·  27g fat  ·  464 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 54% ● Protein 38% ● Net Carbs 8%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
Ground Chicken And Green Bean Stir Fry | Easy Vegetable And Ground Chicken Stir Fry Recipe — Cook! Stacey Cook
Ingredients
  • boneless skinless chicken thigh, minced or finely chopped (or ground chicken thigh) about 4 thighs · 400g
  • green beans, cut into 5 mm pieces about 1 1/3 cups · 130g
  • sesame oil (nuvvula noone) 1 1/2 tbsp · 20g
  • ghee, to finish 1/2 tbsp · 7g
  • mustard seeds 1/3 tsp · 1g
  • cumin seeds 1/2 tsp · 1g
  • whole dried red chillies 2 chillies · 2g
  • fresh curry leaves about 25 leaves · 5g
  • onion, finely chopped 2 1/2 tbsp · 25g
  • ginger, minced 2 tsp · 8g
  • garlic, minced 2 small cloves · 6g
  • green chillies, finely chopped 2 chillies · 10g
  • Guntur red chilli powder 1 1/2 tsp · 4g
  • turmeric powder 1/4 tsp · 1g
  • coriander powder 1 1/2 tsp · 3g
  • garam masala 3/4 tsp · 2g
  • black pepper, freshly ground 1/2 tsp · 1g
  • unsweetened desiccated coconut 2 tbsp · 12g
  • coriander leaves, chopped a handful · 6g
  • lemon juice 2 tsp · 10g
  • salt to taste · 4g
Method
  1. Mince the thighs by hand or pulse in a food processor for 6 to 8 short bursts until coarse (not paste); this is better than store ground chicken, which is often breast-heavy and dries out. Toss the mince with 1/2 tsp salt, the turmeric and 1 tsp of the sesame oil and set aside while you chop (10 minutes).
  2. Heat the remaining sesame oil in a heavy kadai on medium-high. Add mustard seeds and let them crackle, then the cumin, red chillies and curry leaves; fry 15 to 20 seconds until the curry leaves stop spluttering and look crisp.
  3. Add the onion, ginger, garlic and green chillies and fry 3 minutes, stirring, until the onion has softened and starts to colour at the edges and the raw smell of the ginger-garlic is gone.
  4. Turn the heat to high, add the chicken and spread it out. Let it sit 30 seconds, then break it up with a spatula and fry 5 to 6 minutes until all the moisture has cooked off, the chicken looks dry and the oil begins to separate at the edges.
  5. Lower to medium and add the chilli powder, coriander powder and half the pepper. Stir for 30 seconds only (chilli powder scorches fast), then add the beans and 2 tbsp water. Cover and cook 4 minutes until the beans are just tender but still bright, then uncover and fry 1 to 2 minutes to dry out.
  6. Stir in the garam masala, coconut, ghee and the rest of the pepper, cook 1 minute, then finish with lemon juice and coriander. Taste for salt and serve hot.
🌉 US & Bay Area Ingredient Adaptations
  • Fresh curry leaves: Frozen curry leaves straight from the freezer; fresh basil is not a substitute, so omit if you have none
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers (Santa Clara), New India Bazaar (Sunnyvale)
  • Chicken mince: Ground chicken thigh (look for dark-meat grind); or ground lamb for a goat-style keema
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Costco and Whole Foods for ground thigh; halal markets in Sunnyvale and Santa Clara for fresh-minced goat
  • Sesame (gingelly) oil: Avocado oil plus 1/2 tsp white sesame seeds in the tempering for a similar nutty note
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers for Telugu-style nuvvula noone; Trader Joe's or Costco for avocado oil
  • Guntur red chilli powder: Hot Indian red chilli powder; or cayenne plus Kashmiri chilli for colour
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers, New India Bazaar
Make it taste like home
  • Dry the chicken properly before the spices go in: keema fry is judged on texture, and the oil separating at the edges is your cue.
  • Add the chilli powder late and keep the pan on medium so it toasts instead of burning bitter.
  • Keto count: beans are the main carb at about 4 g net per 100 g; do not double them. Onion is measured, not eyeballed.
  • Make-ahead: the keema keeps 3 days in the fridge and reheats well in a dry pan, so cook a double batch of the chicken and add fresh beans each time.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • For heat 2 to 3, use 1 tsp chilli powder and 1 green chilli.
  • Swap the beans for finely chopped zucchini (squeeze it first) for a similar net-carb count.

Kanagarthalu Masala Vepudu (Andhra Mackerel Fry)కనగర్తలు మసాలా వేపుడు

Kanagarthalu (mackerel) is the oily, deeply savoury fish of the Andhra coast. Here it gets a hot, tangy ginger-garlic masala, a quick rest, and a pan-sear in coconut oil until the crust turns dark red and crisp, with curry leaves fried right alongside. No flour coating, no rice flour, nothing but spice.

5.2g net carbs  ·  48g protein  ·  48g fat  ·  660 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 67% ● Protein 30% ● Net Carbs 3%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
Mackerel fish curry|Andhra Style Mackerel fish curry |bangda fish curry|kanagadathalu recipe — kavita srinivas
Ingredients
  • mackerel fillets or steaks (fresh or thawed), skin on, cut into 5 cm pieces 2 large fillets · 500g
  • lemon juice, for the marinade 1 tbsp · 15g
  • ginger, grated 2 tsp · 8g
  • garlic, crushed to a paste 2 to 3 cloves · 8g
  • Guntur red chilli powder 2 tsp · 5g
  • turmeric powder 1/4 tsp · 1g
  • coriander powder 1 1/2 tsp · 3g
  • garam masala 3/4 tsp · 2g
  • black pepper, freshly ground 1/2 tsp · 1g
  • fennel seeds, lightly crushed 1/3 tsp · 1g
  • coconut oil for pan-frying 2 tbsp · 25g
  • fresh curry leaves about 25 leaves · 5g
  • onion, sliced paper-thin, to serve raw a few rings · 15g
  • salt to taste · 4g
Method
  1. Pat the mackerel completely dry with kitchen paper (wet fish steams instead of searing) and make 2 or 3 shallow slits in the skin. Rub with the salt and half the lemon juice and leave for 5 minutes; this also tames the fishy smell.
  2. Mix the ginger, garlic, chilli powder, turmeric, coriander powder, garam masala, pepper, fennel and the rest of the lemon juice with 1 tbsp water into a thick paste, adding drops more water only if it will not spread.
  3. Rub the paste over every surface and into the slits. Rest 15 minutes at room temperature (up to 30 minutes), or refrigerate for up to 3 hours.
  4. Heat the coconut oil in a cast-iron or heavy non-stick pan over medium-high until it shimmers. Lay the fish in skin-side down, away from you, and fry 3 to 4 minutes without moving it, until the underside is deep red-brown and releases easily.
  5. Flip carefully, scatter the curry leaves into the oil around the fish and fry 2 to 3 minutes until the leaves are crisp and the second side is browned and the thickest piece flakes at the centre.
  6. Rest on kitchen paper 1 minute, pile the raw onion rings on top and serve at once with lemon wedges; cauliflower rice with curry-leaf talimpu is the classic keto partner.
🌉 US & Bay Area Ingredient Adaptations
  • Kanagarthalu (Indian mackerel / bangda): Atlantic or Spanish mackerel fillets; or king mackerel steaks; sardines or tilapia fillets also work (shorten cook time)
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: 99 Ranch, Mitsuwa (San Jose), Lunardi's or Whole Foods fish counter; frozen bangda at Patel Brothers and New India Bazaar
  • Fresh curry leaves: Frozen curry leaves straight from the freezer
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers (Santa Clara), New India Bazaar (Sunnyvale)
  • Guntur red chilli powder: Hot Indian chilli powder, or cayenne with a little Kashmiri chilli powder for colour
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers, New India Bazaar
  • Coconut oil: Refined coconut oil for a neutral flavour; avocado oil if you want no coconut note
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Trader Joe's, Costco, Sprouts
Make it taste like home
  • Mackerel is rich and cooks fast; the dark-red crust is the target, so leave it alone for the full 3 to 4 minutes before flipping.
  • No flour, no rice-flour crust: the thin masala paste is what crisps, which keeps the net carbs at the spice and aromatic level only.
  • Keto count: the lemon, garlic and ginger together add about 4 g of carbs across two servings, so keep them measured.
  • Make-ahead: the marinated fish keeps 3 hours in the fridge; cook it only when you are ready to eat.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • For heat 2 to 3, use 2 tsp chilli powder and add 1/2 tsp extra pepper for warmth.
  • If mackerel is too strong for you, use tilapia or catfish fillets with the same marinade, 2 minutes less on each side.

Keto science and tips

ScienceDaily (American Academy of Neurology): today · 5m
Study of nearly 13,000 adults links six common sweeteners, including erythritol and xylitol, to faster cognitive decline  —  Researchers in Brazil followed 12,772 adults for about eight years and estimated sweetener intake from food questionnaires. The highest-intake group had a 62% faster decline in overall cognitive scores than the lowest, about 1.6 years of extra cognitive ageing. Aspartame, saccharin, acesulfame-K, erythritol, sorbitol and xylitol were linked; tagatose was not. It is observational, relies on self-reported diet and cannot show cause.
Takeaway: Erythritol and xylitol are common in low-carb baking and desserts, so this is a data point on sweetener choice, with clear limits on what an observational study shows.
Scientific Reports: yesterday · 6m
A 60-day ketogenic diet study finds hormone and metabolic responses differ by body type and sex  —  Polish researchers followed adults of normal weight, with overweight and with obesity through a 60-day ketogenic diet, measuring blood markers at baseline, day 14, 30 and 60. Beta-hydroxybutyrate rose in every group, but the pace differed, and insulin and HOMA-IR improved mainly in the overweight and obesity groups. Lipid changes were variable. It was a single-arm exploratory study with a small sample, so effects cannot be separated from weight loss or calorie intake.
Takeaway: Shows why two people on the same keto plan can see different ketone, lipid and appetite readings, useful context for your own tracking of the keto-Andhra menu.
News-Medical: yesterday · 5m
Ketones and sleep: review finds mixed effects that depend on diet, age, sex and timing  —  A narrative review in Nutrients (89 peer-reviewed publications, no meta-analysis) looked at how fasting, ketogenic diets and ketone supplements relate to sleep. Evidence is clearest in mice, where acetoacetate raised slow-wave activity. In some healthy adults short carbohydrate restriction was linked to more deep sleep and less REM, and some people report disturbed sleep during early adaptation. Larger trials with objective sleep measures are still needed.
Takeaway: Practical to know that sleep changes during early keto adaptation are reported and that evidence on whether ketones themselves cause them is still thin.
News-Medical (University of Bergen): 2 days ago · 5m
Norwegian two-year trial: very-low-carb, high-saturated-fat diet raised LDL modestly while triglycerides fell and HDL rose  —  Secondary analysis of the CARBFUNC randomised trial (193 adults, followed up to two years, published in eBioMedicine) compared a very-low-carbohydrate, high-saturated-fat diet with two whole-grain-based diets. LDL cholesterol rose 5-10%, mostly in larger particles, apoB did not change appreciably differently between groups, triglycerides fell 20-35% and HDL rose 5-18%. CT scans showed no difference in coronary calcium at 12 months. The researchers say long-term meaning still needs study.
Takeaway: A long, controlled look at how a high-fat, very-low-carb pattern moves several lipid markers at once, useful when reading keto lipid results beyond LDL alone.
Harvard Health Publishing: · 6m
Harvard Health explainer: what magnesium does, how much adults need and what the evidence says about supplements  —  A Harvard Health editor reviews magnesium's roles in nerves, muscles, blood sugar and bone, notes the recommended daily amounts (320 mg for women, 420 mg for men) and says most people can reach them through food. It explains that blood tests and kidney regulation keep levels tight, lists causes of low magnesium, and stresses that many supplement-benefit studies are small or inconsistent.
Takeaway: Magnesium is one of the electrolytes you track on keto; this is a sober, evergreen baseline for food sources versus supplement claims.
