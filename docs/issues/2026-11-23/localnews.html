San José Spotlight: 2 days ago · 4m
San Jose Planning Commission Recommends Strict Data Center Standards and Permit Oversight Shift  —  San Jose's Planning Commission voted unanimously to recommend that the City Council adopt uniform development standards for large data centers, adding a major governance change that shifts initial permit approval authority from the Planning Director to the Planning Commission itself. The proposed rules mandate 100% renewable energy, recycled water rather than drinking water for cooling, noise-mitigation barriers, and prevailing wages for construction workers. Industry representatives objected to layering local commercial linkage and energy reliability fees atop state large-load charges. The City Council is scheduled to vote on Dec. 8, potentially enacting the standards immediately through an urgency ordinance.
Takeaway: Connects your South Bay civic policy tracking with local governance over AI compute power, water, and permitting.
Valley Water: 3 days ago · 3m
Valley Water and Palo Alto Close Baylands Preserve Ahead of Santa Clara County Coastal Flood Warning  —  Valley Water warned Santa Clara County residents on Nov. 23 that a National Weather Service Coastal Flood Warning remains active through Thursday afternoon, driven by exceptionally high king tides and storm surge peaking Tuesday and Wednesday. Rain and gusty winds are also forecast from Tuesday afternoon through Wednesday morning, increasing inundation risk across baylands, wetlands, and shoreline parks. In response, Open Space Rangers proactively closed the Palo Alto Baylands Nature Preserve through Friday. Officials urged drivers and trail users to avoid low-lying bayshore routes and never cross flooded roads.
Takeaway: Directly informs your South Bay neighborhoods and outdoor trails planning during Thanksgiving week.
San José Spotlight: today · 4m
Santa Clara Planning Commission Votes 5-1 Against Rezoning for Pruneridge Golf Course Apartments  —  Santa Clara's Planning Commission voted 5-1 against recommending a General Plan amendment and rezoning for The Greens at Pruneridge, a proposed infill project alongside Pruneridge Golf Club, without acting on its environmental impact report. Demmon Family Partnership revised its 2024 plan from 324 apartments on 4.9 acres to 316 apartments on 3.73 acres—eliminating a second building and adding a 1.74-acre public park while reconfiguring the 9-hole golf course across 25.5 acres. Commissioners cited density, traffic, and questions over whether a decades-old scenic easement preserving green space can legally be modified before the Dec. 1 City Council review.
Takeaway: Relevant to your Santa Clara civic policy and South Bay housing-versus-open-space tracking.
Mountain View Voice: 3 days ago · 3m
NASA Evaluates Leasing Moffett Field Land Near Mountain View for Hyperscale Data Center  —  NASA is soliciting a real estate and investment contractor with hyperscale data center experience to evaluate potential lease sites at the roughly 2,000-acre Ames Research Center at Moffett Field, located in unincorporated Santa Clara County north of Mountain View and U.S. Highway 101. The request asks for decision-ready feasibility and development-readiness assessments, though it does not disclose specific parcel locations on the campus. NASA spokesperson Jeanne Neal said the agency routinely studies potential uses of federal property—including expanding supercomputing data centers—and emphasized that the contractor study is advisory and makes no binding commitment.
Takeaway: Links South Bay regional land-use developments around Mountain View with surging hyperscale compute infrastructure demand.
SFGATE / San José Spotlight: 2 days ago · 4m
South County Completes $395 Million Upper Llagas Creek Flood Protection Project  —  Officials from Valley Water, Morgan Hill, and Santa Clara County marked the completion of major flood protection upgrades along 13.9 miles of Upper Llagas Creek and its tributaries serving Morgan Hill, Gilroy, and San Martin. Conceived more than 70 years ago and backed by local, state, and $80 million in federal funding, the $395 million effort widened channels, added underground culverts, and built a downtown Morgan Hill bypass tunnel. Valley Water says the completed upgrades protect roughly 1,100 homes, 500 businesses, and more than 1,300 acres of agricultural land from a 100-year flood ahead of El Niño winter storms.
Takeaway: Marks a major Santa Clara County flood-resilience milestone as South Bay communities brace for an active El Niño winter.
SFGATE / San José Spotlight: today · 4m
Santa Clara County Nonprofits Expand Food Aid as 31% of Local Residents Face Food Insecurity  —  Santa Clara County food banks and community kitchens are scaling up Thanksgiving meal distributions and mobile pantries as rising Silicon Valley living costs and safety-net cuts under H.R. 1 push more households off CalFresh and Medi-Cal. Loaves & Fishes Family Kitchen spokesperson Stephenie Medina reported that 31% of Santa Clara and San Mateo county residents—and 42% of children—struggle with food insecurity, with the nonprofit expecting annual meal demand to climb from 1.7 million last year to 2 million this year. Martha's Kitchen also reported a 15% demand increase, while Sacred Heart expanded family nutrition support.
Takeaway: Provides concrete socioeconomic data on cost-of-living pressures across Santa Clara County.

Bay Area and California

KQED: 3 days ago · 4m
Thanksgiving Travel Faces Coastal Flood Warning and El Niño King Tides Across Bay Area  —  Forecasters warn that an incoming Pacific storm peaking Tuesday and Wednesday will collide with El Niño-amplified king tides to threaten Bay Area airports, highways, commuter rail, and ferries at the height of Thanksgiving travel. National Weather Service meteorologist Dial Hoang said tides are running roughly a foot higher due to warm El Niño ocean waters and could drive 2 to 2.5 feet of coastal flooding above normal average high tides across bayfront corridors. The warnings follow recent weather-related ground stops at San Francisco International Airport, prompting counties to open free sandbag stations before conditions ease by Thanksgiving Day.
Takeaway: Relevant to your Bay Area regional transit and holiday travel corridor awareness around SFO and bayshore routes.
CalMatters: today · 5m
California Colleges Weigh Building In-House AI Data Centers and Chatbots to Cut Vendor Reliance  —  Even as the University of California announced a new OpenAI deal—following an earlier contract carrying minimum fees of $10 to $30 per user monthly and Cal State's roughly $56 million OpenAI contracts through 2029—California's public higher-education systems are exploring sovereign AI infrastructure. The California Community Colleges system is requesting $21 million from the state next year to build an independent AI network and data centers that administrators say would cost a fraction of commercial licenses and reduce data-security risks. UC and CSU are holding early discussions on potential collaboration.
Takeaway: Ties directly into your AI compute infrastructure, model deployment, and California public-policy interests.
SFGATE: 2 days ago · 3m
Caltrans Shuts Outbound Posey Tube Between Alameda and Oakland for Thanksgiving Week  —  Caltrans closed the Posey Tube carrying Highway 260 traffic out of Alameda toward Oakland from 8 p.m. Sunday, Nov. 22 through 5 p.m. Friday, Nov. 27 to protect the nearly century-old underwater tunnel from king-tide flooding. The U.S. Coast Guard forecast king tides up to 8 feet on Tuesday and Wednesday, while NOAA projected Oakland Estuary water levels at Berth 67 could reach 7.42 feet by Wednesday morning. The inbound Webster Tube remains open, with outbound motorists, cyclists, and pedestrians detoured to the Park Street, Fruitvale Avenue, and High Street bridges.
Takeaway: Key East Bay transit closure to watch if navigating Alameda-Oakland corridors during Thanksgiving week.
Los Angeles Times: 2 days ago · 4m
FCC Preempts California Carrier Rules, Clearing AT&T to Phase Out Copper Landlines  —  The Federal Communications Commission issued an order on Nov. 20 preempting California's decades-old Carrier of Last Resort rules and clearing AT&T to discontinue legacy copper landline service across 360 state wire centers starting in June 2027. The transition affects roughly 184,000 residential and 15,000 business customers, whom AT&T plans to migrate to cellular and wireless alternatives while maintaining 911 access. Consumer advocates warned that wireless networks remain vulnerable during wildfires, earthquakes, and grid outages, and the California Public Utilities Commission said it is evaluating legal options against the federal preemption.
Takeaway: Covers a major federal-state regulatory clash affecting California telecommunications resilience and emergency infrastructure.
Securities.io: 4 days ago · 4m
CPUC Unanimously Approves $456 Million Interim PG&E Rate Increase for 2027  —  California utility regulators unanimously authorized up to $456 million in interim 2027 revenue recovery for PG&E—equal to 51.5% of its $885 million net request and 2.97% of its 2026 revenue requirement—taking effect Jan. 1, 2027. Regulators sized electric hikes smaller and gas reductions larger than PG&E sought to limit overcollection while saving an estimated $13.6 million to $21 million in interest ahead of a final May 2027 rate decision. Consumer Watchdog objected that the vote proceeded while PG&E plans to defer roughly $2 billion in 2027 capital spending; excess collections must be refunded with interest.
Takeaway: Directly impacts Bay Area utility rates and PG&E grid capital-allocation policy heading into 2027.
CalMatters: today · 4m
California and 21 States Sue to Block Federal Rule Ending Mixed-Status HUD Housing Aid  —  California joined 21 other states and Washington D.C. in federal court in Massachusetts on Nov. 23 seeking to freeze a U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development rule slated to take effect Dec. 14. The rule would terminate federal housing vouchers and public housing eligibility for households with mixed immigration statuses, affecting more than 7,000 California families. The states argue HUD exceeded its executive authority by overriding congressional statutes dating back to the Clinton era that provide prorated subsidies strictly for household members with legal status.
Takeaway: Tracks a major federal-state court challenge with direct consequences for California housing affordability and municipal homelessness.
