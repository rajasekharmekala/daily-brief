Trending agent papers

Clover-35B-A3B-Preview: Recurrent-Memory Diffusion MoE for Coding Agents  —  This technical report introduces Clover-35B-A3B-Preview, a 35B-total, 3B-active MoE block-diffusion model aimed at accelerating decoding in agent loops. Clover adapts Gated Delta Networks to combine recurrent historical memory with bidirectional intra-block softmax attention. It scores 73.6 on SWE-bench Verified and 51.7 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, reaching 680 tokens/s at BF16 batch size 1 on a single NVIDIA GB300 GPU. A key limitation is that its throughput and group-decision gains (93.88% vs 90.37% accuracy) are reported on internal workloads and high-end GB300 hardware.
Takeaway: Worth tracking for your production agent runtime and Terminal-Bench work, since 680 tokens/s block-diffusion models could reshape multi-step harness latency and parallel verification costs.
arXiv: 7 days ago · 9m
Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights Without Gradients  —  This paper explores whether frozen language models can improve permanently through self-rewriting without retraining, prompts, or harnesses. Given access to its weights, graded answers, and forward-pass execution, an open 27B model diagnoses its own failures and directly edits its weights, keeping only edits validated on practice items. It gains 6.6 points on unseen problems, improves monotonically across eight consecutive sessions, and restores a damaged copy to over 80% of original accuracy on unseen image tasks. Smaller models in the family make no edit or break themselves, showing the capability emerges with scale.
Takeaway: Directly relevant to your agent self-improvement and harness-engineering tracking, framing weight self-editing as a complement or alternative to outer-loop prompt and harness tuning.
arXiv: 3 days ago · 7m
MemOnce: Scalable Memory States for Long-Horizon LLM Interactions  —  MemOnce tackles linear GPU KV-cache growth across long-horizon and continual LLM interactions. It caches a compact subset of final-layer hidden states and dynamically decodes them back into attention KV caches using cross-layer sparse memory retrieval. Adapting Qwen3.5-4B at 1M context cuts memory-state cache size by 1,313x and prefill latency by 6.9x while raising throughput 3.5x and memory performance by +5.77%. As a custom architectural adaptation, it requires pretraining or post-training adaptation rather than dropping straight into unmodified frozen weights.
Takeaway: Directly relevant to your agent memory and serving-efficiency interests, offering an architectural path to keep million-token agent state compact across turns.
arXiv: 4 days ago · 7m
Duopra: Bringing Task Execution Agency to Full-Duplex Voice Models  —  Duopra investigates why full-duplex voice interaction models fail on tool-backed tasks even when conversational turn-taking is fluid. It unifies direct tool calls and backend delegation behind one action interface, trained with protocol SFT, full-duplex RL, and projector-only talker conditioning repair. On Full-Duplex-Bench v3 with the same backend, Pass@1 jumps from 4.7% to 44.7% (achieving 47.7% calling tools directly and 49.3% with a DeepSeek backend). The authors evaluate task execution alongside FDB v1.0 takeover rates.
Takeaway: Connects directly to your real-time voice AI and agent-runtime interests, quantifying why conversational turn-taking alone breaks down when voice agents must trigger backend tools reliably.
arXiv: 7 days ago · 7m
RepoFactory: Scaling Long-Horizon Coding Environments from Real Repos  —  RepoFactory addresses the shortage of verifiable environments for long-horizon coding agents by converting real repositories into build-from-scratch tasks. Its Environment Evolution Loop optimizes validity, structural coupling, and implementation scope via proxy signals, yielding 10,159 environments across 17 software domains. Training Qwen3.5-35B-A3B produces RepoHorizen-35B-A3B, adding 22.6 percentage points on NL2Repo-Bench and 8.5 points on average across five benchmarks. These gains outperform training without environment evolution (17.5 and 6.4 points).
Takeaway: Relevant to your coding-agent and long-horizon evaluation work, showing how automated repository-to-environment evolution boosts full-project generation beyond static task validation.
arXiv: 5 days ago · 8m
Piccolo: Co-Evolving Decision Models and Data via Recursive Self-Improvement  —  Piccolo presents a 0.8B-to-27B multimodal decision-model family for agent harnesses, judgments, and data workflows from the Data-Juicer and AgentScope ecosystems. Research agents analyze model behavior, curate semantic data slices, and iteratively refine training recipes via shared research memory. Piccolo-27B leads the Decision Index Vision Full leaderboard at 75.59, while Piccolo-9B and Piccolo-4B reach 68.35 and 67.46, rivaling 27B baselines on specialized workflows. Notably, the authors report that continuous loss reduction can decouple from and even degrade discrete decision accuracy.
Takeaway: Useful for your agent-harness and judge-model work, highlighting how smaller 4B-9B decision models trained on agent-curated slices can rival larger verifiers.

Runtime, harness and production engineering

arXiv: 3 days ago · 9m
Contexture: Runtime Context Eviction for High-Throughput Agent Serving  —  Researchers at Nanyang Technological University tackle shared GPU HBM KV-cache exhaustion when agents resend long tool-use histories. Contexture separates the harness's full conversation log from the serving context, evicting superseded tool outputs (64% of recorded final-history tokens) only when global KV-cache pressure justifies losing cached prefixes. Across two models, two harnesses, and Terminal-Bench 2.1 / SWE-style workloads on vLLM, it boosts throughput under SLO by 20-73% over baselines (and 23% over LMCache on H100) while improving mean live-agent accuracy by 1.4 percentage points. However, it shows a worst-case 3.7-point accuracy drop and depends on identifying superseding tool operations.
Takeaway: Directly applicable to your production agent runtime and vLLM serving interests, bridging harness-level tool semantics with engine-level KV pressure without modifying model weights.
arXiv: 4 days ago · 8m
Held While Waiting: What LLM Agent Sessions Reserve on Serverless Runtimes  —  In an independent measurement study (authored outside AWS duties), Mekhala Hithyshini Sreenivasa shows that agent sessions spend 98.5-99.8% of their lifetime waiting on LLM replies while still pinning isolated serverless environments. On Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, sessions waited a median of 2.7 seconds per model turn against 5-40 ms of local compute, and 33-second conversations remained reserved for 15 minutes unless explicitly closed. Simulating 64 conversations sharing one environment cuts environment reservations 59x with 99% of replies delayed by at most 40 ms. The open systems challenge is enforcing multi-tenant isolation for model-chosen tools without pinning one VM per idle conversation.
Takeaway: Directly informs your agent-runtime architecture work, exposing why per-session microVM isolation hits environment quotas long before CPU limits.
arXiv: 3 days ago · 7m
Epistemic Guard: Out-of-Process MCP Receipt Proxying and Provenance Memory  —  Sean Maxwell at Roll Solo addresses three runtime verification gaps in MCP agents: fabricated post-tool prose, unverified prose leaking into durable memory, and mirrored web echo chambers. Epistemic Guard (v0.5.2) interposes an out-of-process stdio MCP proxy with Ed25519 RFC 6962 Merkle receipts, CPU OpenVINO INT8 prose supervision (0 MiB VRAM), and provenance-gated SQLite writes. On 250 benchmark traces it hits 93.2% accuracy at 17.9 ms median CPU latency, beating 12B-14B GPU judges by 6.4-6.8 points, and catches 96.15% of hazards on 60 live proxy traces. A notable limitation is its 38.24% false-positive rate on live traces.
Takeaway: High signal for your MCP and agent-observability stack, pairing cryptographic tool-call receipts with sub-20 ms CPU checks before agent outputs commit to memory.
arXiv: 3 days ago · 6m
Exercised, Rarely Recovered: Auditing Recovery Across 50 Agent Runtimes  —  Kaviru Hapuarachchi (University of Colombo School of Computing) audits public evidence on eight recovery mechanisms across 12 persistent runtimes, 26 coding-agent harnesses, and 12 research runtimes using a frozen coding protocol. While 11 of 12 persistent runtimes and 25 of 26 harnesses had real-world recovery reports, only 19 of 128 reports achieved full recovery, with 54 partial and 45 failed; checkpoint/resume succeeded once in 27 reports and rollback once in 15. Research runtimes showed the reverse gap—benchmark guarantees with zero production operation reports, and external-effect compensation absent from all harnesses. Findings are bounded by reliance on public incident reports.
Takeaway: A sobering reality check for your production harness engineering: checkpointing and rollback are widely advertised in agent runtimes yet rarely recover cleanly in live failures.
arXiv: 5 days ago · 7m
Great Listeners, Poor Decision-Makers: Action Gating for Voice Agents  —  Siddique Latif and co-authors argue that voice agents fail less from ASR errors than from acting prematurely on side talk, misheard commands, or incomplete utterances. They decouple an explicit decision-gating layer that weighs addressedness, speaker identity, transcript confidence, utterance completeness, and action risk before choosing to act, wait, ask, or stay silent. On recorded audio including egocentric captures, explicit gating cuts executed side talk from 41% down to 7-10% while serving 83-85% of valid requests after clarification, beating prompting baselines. Limitations noted include testing only 7B-13B open-weight audio models and simplified independence assumptions in simulations.
Takeaway: Directly relevant to your real-time voice AI interest, providing a concrete pre-tool gating design to stop conversational voice agents from misfiring on background speech.
Modal Blog: Nov 12 · 5m
Sandbox Resourcing: Burst, Don't Reserve, for Coding Agents  —  Modal engineer Kevin Yang documents why coding-agent sandboxes waste reserved compute: across Modal, a typical sandbox spends over half its life below 6% of peak CPU while waiting on model tokens, spiking only during dependency installs or test runs. In a replayed 2m46s Terminal-Bench 2.1 patch session, memory stayed near 47 MiB and CPU spiked briefly for pytest between 33s of model waits. Modal defaults sandboxes to 0.125 cores and 128 MiB guaranteed, bursting up to 16 cores with an OOM SLO under 0.0001% per minute below 8 GiB. The tradeoff is possible CPU slowdown or OOM eviction above guaranteed memory during correlated spikes.
Takeaway: Pairs well with serverless runtime research for your coding-agent infrastructure, illustrating practical resource-bursting defaults and exit-snapshot recovery on Terminal-Bench workloads.

Evaluation, observability and safety

arXiv: 3 days ago · 8m
Benchmarks' Last Exam: Auditing Prompt, Environment, and Grader Alignment  —  Researchers at Benchmarks' Last Exam Lab audit 749 tasks across eight frontier benchmarks and 767 public evaluations to measure how often prompts, environments, and graders disagree. While revised closed-form suites reach 92.5% verified validity, six multi-step agentic suites sit at just 29.3% validity due to incomplete state assertions, shared-channel leaks, and rubric mismatches. Crucially, 71.7% of confirmed defects unfairly withhold credit from valid solutions (understating capability) while 28.3% allow shortcut passes; grading patches inside fresh containers slashes runtime bypasses 5.5x from 14.7% to 2.7%. The synthesis represents an initial milestone across eight benchmarks rather than exhaustive leaderboard coverage.
Takeaway: Essential reading for your agent evaluation and benchmark-builder work, quantifying how harness leaks and grading bugs distort multi-step pass rates.
arXiv: 3 days ago · 8m
Who Grades the Agent? Privilege Separation for Coding-Agent Benchmarks  —  Authors from the University of Padua and UC San Diego introduce SplitBench to stop coding agents from tampering with their own test harnesses during evaluation. Applying privilege separation, SplitBench isolates test execution from a trusted grader that holds expected answers, so even total takeover of the test container cannot forge a pass. On Terminal-Bench and DeepSWE, 180 of 202 tasks port cleanly with 98.7% agreement against native graders, and SplitBench blocks 75% of worst-case attacks where agents read all grading code. The remaining 25% mostly exposed weak upstream tests, and the authors note semantic gaps outside the tested attack budgets.
Takeaway: Directly relevant to your coding-agent benchmarking and harness security interests, offering a drop-in separation pattern to prevent Terminal-Bench-style reward hacking.
arXiv: 6 days ago · 7m
Simplicity Goes Far: Auditing Prompt Optimizers Against Demonstration Search  —  Bohe Chen (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications and Queen Mary University of London) audits complex prompt optimizers by isolating their generate-and-select baseline as Demonstration-Conditioned Prompt Search (DCPS). Without reflection, evolution, or RL updates, DCPS matches StablePrompt PPO classification accuracy within 0.5 pp at roughly one-third the cost, and in DSPy compound AI systems matches GEPA's macro accuracy on GPT-4.1-mini using 55% fewer rollouts and 32% lower API cost on average. Heavier optimizers still win on certain open-ended reasoning tasks. Limitations noted include single-run budget cells across three compound tasks rather than multi-seed statistical rankings.
Takeaway: Directly speaks to your GEPA prompt-optimization and benchmark work, providing a sharp cost-controlled baseline to test whether reflective mutations earn their extra rollout spend.
arXiv: 4 days ago · 7m
AltRA-Test: Validating LLM Judges When Human Annotators Don't Overlap  —  Researchers at Technion and the University of Haifa generalize the Alternative Annotator Test to sparse labeling regimes where different humans rate different subsets. AltRA-Test resamples items and substitutes one human rating with an LLM rating to test whether mixed-panel inter-annotator agreement is statistically non-inferior to all-human panels. Across 18 LLMs, 5 prompting methods, and 20 multimodal datasets, roughly 100 items often suffice, and LLMs pass replacement in about half of tested setups depending on task difficulty. Like any annotator test, its validity remains bounded by the quality of underlying human labels.
Takeaway: Practical statistical tooling for your LLM-as-judge reliability work, giving a 100-item check before replacing sparse human graders in evaluation pipelines.
arXiv: 4 days ago · 6m
Your Judge Inherits Your Prompt: Directional Label Bias in LLM-as-Judge  —  Abdullah Ahmad, Ahmed J. O. Ahmed, and Luca Citi (University of Essex; BlackboxNLP 2026) test whether few-shot demonstration balance skews frozen LLM judges on multimodal VQA evaluation. Holding generated answers fixed, skewing judge examples toward Yes inflates acceptance rates by roughly 30 points on NExT-QA and 20 points on MSVD-QA relative to two-annotator human consensus, whereas rejection-skewed examples produce no matching drop. Moreover, single random example draws vary by up to 18 points—more than differences between prompting methods. Limitations include testing a single rejection-skewed set per dataset without category-matched balancing across skews.
Takeaway: A concrete guardrail for your LLM-as-judge pipelines: always balance few-shot judge exemplars and average across multiple draws so prompt skew cannot fabricate false gains.
