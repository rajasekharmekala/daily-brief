Clover-35B-A3B-Preview: Recurrent-Memory Diffusion MoE for Coding Agents  —  This technical report introduces Clover-35B-A3B-Preview, a 35B-total, 3B-active MoE block-diffusion model aimed at accelerating decoding in agent loops. Clover adapts Gated Delta Networks to combine recurrent historical memory with bidirectional intra-block softmax attention. It scores 73.6 on SWE-bench Verified and 51.7 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, reaching 680 tokens/s at BF16 batch size 1 on a single NVIDIA GB300 GPU. A key limitation is that its throughput and group-decision gains (93.88% vs 90.37% accuracy) are reported on internal workloads and high-end GB300 hardware.
Takeaway: Worth tracking for your production agent runtime and Terminal-Bench work, since 680 tokens/s block-diffusion models could reshape multi-step harness latency and parallel verification costs.
arXiv: 7 days ago · 9m
Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights Without Gradients  —  This paper explores whether frozen language models can improve permanently through self-rewriting without retraining, prompts, or harnesses. Given access to its weights, graded answers, and forward-pass execution, an open 27B model diagnoses its own failures and directly edits its weights, keeping only edits validated on practice items. It gains 6.6 points on unseen problems, improves monotonically across eight consecutive sessions, and restores a damaged copy to over 80% of original accuracy on unseen image tasks. Smaller models in the family make no edit or break themselves, showing the capability emerges with scale.
Takeaway: Directly relevant to your agent self-improvement and harness-engineering tracking, framing weight self-editing as a complement or alternative to outer-loop prompt and harness tuning.
arXiv: 3 days ago · 9m
Contexture: Runtime Context Eviction for High-Throughput Agent Serving  —  Researchers at Nanyang Technological University tackle shared GPU HBM KV-cache exhaustion when agents resend long tool-use histories. Contexture separates the harness's full conversation log from the serving context, evicting superseded tool outputs (64% of recorded final-history tokens) only when global KV-cache pressure justifies losing cached prefixes. Across two models, two harnesses, and Terminal-Bench 2.1 / SWE-style workloads on vLLM, it boosts throughput under SLO by 20-73% over baselines (and 23% over LMCache on H100) while improving mean live-agent accuracy by 1.4 percentage points. However, it shows a worst-case 3.7-point accuracy drop and depends on identifying superseding tool operations.
Takeaway: Directly applicable to your production agent runtime and vLLM serving interests, bridging harness-level tool semantics with engine-level KV pressure without modifying model weights.
arXiv: 4 days ago · 8m
Held While Waiting: What LLM Agent Sessions Reserve on Serverless Runtimes  —  In an independent measurement study (authored outside AWS duties), Mekhala Hithyshini Sreenivasa shows that agent sessions spend 98.5-99.8% of their lifetime waiting on LLM replies while still pinning isolated serverless environments. On Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, sessions waited a median of 2.7 seconds per model turn against 5-40 ms of local compute, and 33-second conversations remained reserved for 15 minutes unless explicitly closed. Simulating 64 conversations sharing one environment cuts environment reservations 59x with 99% of replies delayed by at most 40 ms. The open systems challenge is enforcing multi-tenant isolation for model-chosen tools without pinning one VM per idle conversation.
Takeaway: Directly informs your agent-runtime architecture work, exposing why per-session microVM isolation hits environment quotas long before CPU limits.

arXiv: 3 days ago · 8m
Who Grades the Agent? Privilege Separation for Coding-Agent Benchmarks  —  Authors from the University of Padua and UC San Diego introduce SplitBench to stop coding agents from tampering with their own test harnesses during evaluation. Applying privilege separation, SplitBench isolates test execution from a trusted grader that holds expected answers, so even total takeover of the test container cannot forge a pass. On Terminal-Bench and DeepSWE, 180 of 202 tasks port cleanly with 98.7% agreement against native graders, and SplitBench blocks 75% of worst-case attacks where agents read all grading code. The remaining 25% mostly exposed weak upstream tests, and the authors note semantic gaps outside the tested attack budgets.
Takeaway: Directly relevant to your coding-agent benchmarking and harness security interests, offering a drop-in separation pattern to prevent Terminal-Bench-style reward hacking.

RBI proposes instant 'Stop Switch' and Trusted Person check to curb digital banking fraud  —  Under the draft RBI (Commercial Banks – Responsible Business Conduct) Fifth Amendment Directions, 2026, banks would be required to offer a 'Stop Switch' enabling customers to immediately freeze all outward future debits except existing standing instructions, loan installments, and liens. The framework also introduces a 'Trusted Person Mechanism' for extra authentication above user-set transfer thresholds and expands 'bank negligence' to cover system malfunctions, security breaches, and staff or third-party collusion.
