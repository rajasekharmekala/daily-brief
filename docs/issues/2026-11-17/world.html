IEA says global oil supply swung back to surplus in October as Gulf exports recovered  —  The International Energy Agency reported on November 13 that global oil supplies exceeded demand by almost 2 million barrels a day in October, reversing earlier deficit projections as Persian Gulf exports recovered faster than consumption. The agency expects global supply to average 105 million barrels a day in the fourth quarter, 1.7 million above demand, while accelerating the release of 71 million barrels of remaining emergency stockpiles through February. OPEC+ output reached 41 million barrels a day, or 97% of prewar levels, helping pull Brent crude from April's $126 peak down toward $95, though tanker attacks remain a lingering risk.
Takeaway: Connects your global energy and Strait of Hormuz tracking to hard IEA supply-demand balances shaping crude prices and emergency reserve releases.

Wholesale inflation climbs to 12.3% in October, intensifying RBI rate pressure  —  Official Ministry of Commerce and Industry data released on November 16 showed India's Wholesale Price Index (WPI) rose to 12.3% in October 2026, marking a second straight month of double-digit wholesale inflation after September's 11.4%. Energy drove the spike: fuel and power jumped nearly 33% and crude petroleum and natural gas hit 70.8% (aided by a 2025 low base), while food inflation reached a 22-month high of 8.3% and manufactured goods rose 9.5%. Coming days after retail CPI breached the RBI's 6% upper band, economists warned that pass-through to consumer prices makes an interest rate hike nearly certain. Because the revamped WPI series only starts in April 2024, longer historical comparisons remain limited.
Takeaway: Directly tracks your India economy and RBI policy thread, as consecutive double-digit WPI prints alongside a 6%-plus CPI reading heighten the odds of tighter monetary policy.

India posts record $89.99 billion in October exports as overall trade gap narrows  —  India's combined merchandise and services exports rose 29.9% year-on-year to $89.99 billion in October 2026, up from $69.27 billion a year earlier, according to Commerce Ministry data. Merchandise shipments surged 45.06% to an all-time monthly high of $49.47 billion, supported by market diversification and new trade pacts with the UK and New Zealand. However, goods imports climbed to $89.35 billion amid West Asia energy disruptions, widening the merchandise trade deficit to a one-year high of $39.88 billion versus $32.25 billion forecast. An estimated $40.51 billion in services exports against $20.72 billion in services imports cushioned the goods gap, narrowing the overall deficit to $20.09 billion, though October services figures remain preliminary estimates.
Takeaway: Relevant to your India economy interest, highlighting how a strong services surplus and record goods shipments offset elevated energy import bills.

SEBI forms Ashish Chauhan-led panel to simplify IPO, FPO and QIP rules  —  SEBI has formed a Primary Market Advisory Committee (PMAC) sub-group to review its Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements (ICDR) Regulations, 2018, aiming to ease capital-market access and remove duplicative disclosures. Chaired by NSE managing director and CEO Ashish Chauhan, the panel includes representatives from BSE, ICAI, ICSI, investment banks, law firms, and industry body CII. Its mandate covers issuer eligibility across IPOs, FPOs, preferential issues, and qualified institutional placements (QIPs), alongside promoter and pre-issue lock-in rules, allotment procedures, offer-document disclosures, and pricing frameworks such as floor prices and price bands. The sub-group can co-opt experts, and its recommendations will go to the full PMAC before any regulatory changes take effect.

Andhra Pradesh plans Investment Protection Act to shield projects across government transitions  —  Andhra Pradesh plans to introduce an Investment Protection Act (IPA) to stop future political transitions from altering business contracts, industrial land allocations, or approved incentives. Announced by IT Minister Nara Lokesh at the 31st CII Partnership Summit in Visakhapatnam, the bill responds to post-2019 policy reversals around Amaravati, where roughly 29,000 farmers had pooled 33,000 acres and contract reviews prompted the World Bank ($300 million), AIIB ($200 million), and a Singapore consortium to withdraw. Although work and multilateral funding resumed after June 2024, the bill is not yet drafted and faces legislative introduction in the upcoming Assembly session.

U.S. Chamber of Commerce: 4 days ago · 6m
USCIS H-1B extension deference comment window closes with U.S. Chamber warning of RFE surge  —  Public comments closed on November 13 for DHS's October 14 proposed rule (Docket No. USCIS-2026-0529) to rescind 8 C.F.R. 214.1(c)(5), which requires USCIS officers to give deference to prior Form I-129 approvals when adjudicating unchanged work-visa extensions including H-1B, L-1, and O-1 petitions. In its formal filing, the U.S. Chamber of Commerce cited USCIS data showing that after deference was suspended in October 2017, H-1B continuing-employment denial rates climbed from 5% in FY 2017 to 12% in FY 2018-2019 before dropping to 2% in FY 2021 upon reinstatement, while RFE rates jumped from 21.4% to 40.2%. The rule remains a proposal and existing deference standards stay active until a final rule takes effect.
Takeaway: Directly tracks USCIS and H-1B regulatory policy updates under your US-India corridor interest.
