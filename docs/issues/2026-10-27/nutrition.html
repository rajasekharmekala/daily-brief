Badam-Perugu Steamed Cakes with Kobbari Pachadi

Breakfast · 5.8g net carbs · 13g protein · 25m
Soft idli-style almond and yogurt cakes, steamed in the pressure cooker and topped with a crackling curry-leaf talimpu, with a fresh green-chilli coconut pachadi. They are not fermented idli, but they scratch the same itch.
Ingredients
  • almond flour, blanched (40g)
  • eggs, whisked until frothy (100g)
  • plain whole-milk yogurt (60g)
  • ghee, for greasing and talimpu (10g)
  • salt (3g)
  • mustard seeds (2g)
  • cumin seeds (1g)
  • curry leaves (3g)
  • green chilli, chopped (cakes) (6g)
  • fresh or frozen grated coconut (pachadi) (30g)
  • green chilli (pachadi) (6g)
  • ginger (pachadi) (4g)
  • lemon juice (pachadi) (5g)
Method
  1. Whisk the eggs hard for 2 minutes until frothy and pale, then whisk in the yogurt, almond flour, salt and chopped green chilli. Rest 5 minutes while the cooker heats.
  2. Grease two ramekins or idli moulds generously with half the ghee and fill each three-quarters full.
  3. Boil 2 cm of water in the cooker, set the ramekins on a stand, close the lid WITHOUT the weight and steam 12 to 14 minutes until a knife comes out clean and the tops spring back.
  4. Meanwhile blitz coconut, green chilli, ginger, lemon juice, 3 tbsp water and a pinch of salt into a coarse pachadi.
  5. Heat the remaining ghee until shimmering, add mustard and let it pop, then cumin and curry leaves until the leaves stop spluttering (about 20 seconds). Pour over the cakes and pachadi.
  6. Rest 2 minutes, loosen with a knife and serve hot.

Gongura Royyala Kura (Sorrel Prawn Masala)

Lunch · 8.7g net carbs · 23g protein · 30m
Prawns seared hot, then folded into a thick olive-dark gongura masala and finished with a sizzling garlic and red-chilli talimpu. The sourness comes entirely from the leaf, so there is no tamarind and no jaggery.
Ingredients
  • medium prawns, peeled and deveined (thawed if frozen) (300g)
  • gongura (sorrel) leaves, picked from stems, washed and fully dried, chopped (120g)
  • groundnut oil (22g)
  • sesame oil, for the final talimpu (6g)
  • onion, finely chopped (30g)
  • green chillies, slit (12g)
  • garlic, 4 g crushed with the ginger and 3 g sliced for the talimpu (7g)
  • ginger (4g)
  • Guntur karam (red chilli powder) (4.5g)
  • dried red chillies for the talimpu (1.5g)
  • coriander powder (3g)
  • turmeric powder (0.8g)
  • curry leaves (2g)
  • cumin seeds (1g)
  • mustard seeds (1g)
  • cinnamon stick, a 1 cm piece (0.4g)
  • green cardamom pods, lightly crushed (0.5g)
  • salt (3g)
Method
  1. Pat the prawns completely dry. Toss with the turmeric, a third of the salt and half of the Guntur karam; set aside while you prepare the gongura.
  2. Strip the gongura leaves from the stems, wash, and spin or pat them very dry (wet leaves stew instead of frying and dilute the sourness). Chop coarsely.
  3. Heat 1 tablespoon of the groundnut oil in a kadai over high heat until it shimmers. Sear the prawns in two batches, 45 to 60 seconds per side, just until they curl and turn pink on the outside. They will finish cooking later. Tip them onto a plate.
  4. Lower the heat to medium. Add the remaining groundnut oil, the cumin, mustard seeds, cinnamon and cardamom. When the mustard stops popping (about 20 seconds) add the onion, the slit green chillies and the curry leaves; cook 3 minutes until the onion is soft with golden edges.
  5. Stir in the crushed garlic and ginger for 45 seconds, then the remaining Guntur karam and the coriander powder with a splash of water so the spices do not burn. Add all the gongura and a pinch of salt. Cook 4 to 5 minutes, mashing with the back of a spoon, until the leaves collapse into a dark olive pulp and the oil starts to ooze back out at the edges.
  6. Add 3 tablespoons of water, scrape the pan and simmer 1 minute. Return the prawns and their juices, toss to coat, cover and cook on medium-low for 2 to 3 minutes, until the prawns are opaque and firm. Do not go longer or they turn rubbery.
  7. For the talimpu, heat the sesame oil in a small pan over medium heat. Add the dried red chillies and sliced garlic; when the garlic turns golden (about 30 seconds) pour the sizzling oil over the curry. Rest 2 minutes with the lid on, then taste and add salt or a squeeze more green chilli.

Chicken 65 (Air-Fryer or Shallow-Fry, Almond-Flour Coating)

Dinner · 7.3g net carbs · 45g protein · 28m
Crisp-edged, brick-red cubes of thigh with a garlicky curry-leaf and green-chilli tempering. The coating is yogurt, egg and almond flour: no maida, no cornflour, no sugar.
Ingredients
  • boneless skinless chicken thigh, cut into 2.5 cm cubes (400g)
  • plain whole-milk yogurt (curd) (40g)
  • egg, lightly beaten (25g)
  • almond flour (plain, finely ground, no added starch) (20g)
  • ginger, crushed (8g)
  • garlic, crushed (marinade) (10g)
  • Kashmiri or Guntur red chilli powder (marinade) (5g)
  • turmeric powder (1g)
  • coriander seed, ground (1.5g)
  • cumin seed, ground (0.7g)
  • cinnamon and cloves, ground (garam masala base) (0.8g)
  • black pepper, ground (1g)
  • lemon juice (marinade) (10g)
  • fresh curry leaves, finely chopped (marinade) (2g)
  • salt (3g)
  • avocado oil, for the air fryer or shallow fry (15g)
  • sesame oil, for the tempering (7g)
  • garlic, chopped (tempering) (6g)
  • green chillies, slit (tempering) (10g)
  • fresh curry leaves, whole (tempering) (3g)
  • red chilli powder (tempering paste) (2g)
  • lemon juice (finish) (5g)
  • coriander leaves, chopped (3g)
Method
  1. In a bowl, mix the yogurt, egg, ginger, 10 g crushed garlic, 5 g chilli powder, turmeric, ground coriander, ground cumin, the cinnamon-clove pinch, pepper, lemon juice, chopped curry leaves and 2 g of the salt into a loose paste. Add the chicken cubes and mix well. Sprinkle the almond flour over and fold until every cube has a thick, clinging coat. Rest 10 minutes (longer in the fridge is better, see tips). Preheat the air fryer to 190 C / 375 F.
  2. While the chicken rests, mix the tempering paste: 2 g chilli powder, the last 1 g of salt, and 2 tbsp water in a small bowl. Chop 6 g garlic and slit the green chillies.
  3. Air-fryer route: brush the basket lightly with 1/2 tbsp avocado oil, spread the cubes in one layer without crowding (cook in two batches if needed), and drizzle or spray the remaining oil over the tops. Air fry 8 minutes, flip, then 3 to 4 more minutes until deep golden and a cube reads 74 C / 165 F inside. For the last 2 minutes add a sprig of curry leaves and the slit chillies to the basket.
  4. Shallow-fry route: heat all 15 g avocado oil in a wide nonstick or cast-iron pan on medium-high until it shimmers. Lay in the cubes in one layer, leaving gaps. Cook 3 to 4 minutes undisturbed until the underside is golden and releases easily, flip, and cook 3 to 4 minutes more; work in batches so they fry instead of steam, until a cube reads 74 C / 165 F inside.
  5. Heat the sesame oil in a wok or kadai over medium-high. Add the chopped garlic, the remaining curry leaves and the slit green chillies and stir-fry 20 to 30 seconds until the curry leaves turn crisp (do not brown the garlic). Lower the heat, add the chilli paste and cook 10 seconds until it smells fragrant.
  6. Add the hot chicken, toss on high heat for 30 to 45 seconds until every piece is glossy and red, then finish with the lemon juice and coriander. Serve immediately so the crust stays crisp.
