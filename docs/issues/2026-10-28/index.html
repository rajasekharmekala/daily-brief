arXiv: 3 days ago · 5m
Memory Tax: many dedicated agent-memory systems lose to naive RAG over raw history  —  Asks whether building agent memory beats plain retrieval over raw history. A large evaluation across several benchmarks and model backbones finds many dedicated memory systems underperform naive RAG in many cases. The authors split memory's net value into 'memory benefit' and 'memory tax', where reorganising or distilling facts can also remove or obscure evidence. From the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign; about 120 upvotes (123 when checked on 28 Oct) on Hugging Face's Oct 25-31 weekly list. The abstract gives no headline percentages and says code and results will be released later.
Takeaway: Before adding or keeping a memory layer in a long-horizon agent, benchmark it against a raw-history RAG baseline in your evaluation suite and track what the memory step discards.
arXiv: 3 days ago · 6m
DynaFlow: training a main agent to write its own multi-agent workflows  —  Fixed multi-agent topologies adapt poorly to varied tasks. DynaFlow lets a main agent decide when to collaborate and generate programs that coordinate fixed sub-agents (sequential or parallel, with verification), trained by supervised fine-tuning on distilled trajectories and then filtered examples. On six benchmarks (maths, science QA, multi-hop retrieval) it reaches 88.08% overall accuracy in Forced mode, 10.90 points above the strongest baseline with the same Qwen3.5-27B backbone. National University of Singapore; 203 upvotes and 129 GitHub stars on the Hugging Face list. Benchmarks are reasoning and retrieval, not production tool-use.
Takeaway: Shows orchestration as learned, traceable code rather than a fixed graph, which matters for multi-agent runtime design and for evaluating information flow between stages.
arXiv: 3 days ago · 12m
CTXAgent: letting the model manage its own context instead of threshold compaction  —  Harnesses usually compact history once a token threshold is crossed. This paper gives the model two tools (compact, curate) and one paragraph of guidance, then compares seven frontier models on 35-212-turn sessions mixing coding, SciCode, SWE-bench Pro and Terminal-Bench 4.0 tasks with planted facts. With Claude Opus 5.5 the self-managed harness matches Claude Code on task success at 34% lower cost than its 200k-window setting and 49% lower than its default. Savings vary: some models compact so often that other costs rise. Agent, benchmark and results are released.
Takeaway: Direct evidence for your context-engineering and harness thread: it measures compaction policy against cost and success, and releases a benchmark you could reuse to test your own compaction rules.
arXiv: yesterday · 12m
Your Policy Is Secretly a Harness: why harness comparisons with a fixed model mislead  —  Harnesses are often judged by running an already trained model under them, which assumes learned behaviour transfers. The authors show under KL-regularized control that a trained policy carries its harness's preferences, so return equals the harness ceiling minus the policy's mismatch. In MiniWoB++, reordering observation fields lowers a trained model's success from 85.0% to 19.2%. On Terminal-Bench Lite, ceiling minus mismatch, estimated with a stronger stand-in model, reproduces the accuracy ranking of coding-agent harnesses. Code is public; evidence covers two environments.
Takeaway: Warns that swapping tools, prompts or observation formats in an agent harness can break a tuned model, which matters for how you design harness A/B evaluations and prompt-optimization comparisons.
arXiv: 5 days ago · 8m
Trial by Trace: a benchmark that scores agent harnesses, not just models  —  Most agent benchmarks score models, so harness faults go unmeasured. TraceTrial-Bench groups harness reliability into seven components (context, execution, governance, lifecycle, observability, tools, verification) with 95 mechanism-oriented tasks, execution tracing and an LLM-based failure-attribution step. Across 4 harnesses, 9 models and 3,420 runs, harness scores range from 59.8% to 72.0%, and 31.1% of runs succeed despite failing at least one harness criterion. Authors are at Chinese Academy of Sciences institutes, Tsinghua and Beijing Institute of Technology. Scale is small: 4 harnesses, 95 tasks.
Takeaway: A concrete rubric for your harness and observability work: task success alone hides harness failures, so per-component trace checks are worth adding next to outcome metrics.
arXiv: yesterday · 10m
GEPA-Curator: choosing which agent failures a prompt optimizer learns from  —  Reflective prompt optimizers such as GEPA learn from failed rollouts, but failures can come from harness limits, randomness or noisy judges rather than the prompt. A UC Berkeley-led team adds a curator that filters noisy tasks out of each minibatch and retrieves a prior successful trajectory as contrast context. On GoBrowse, ALFWorld, AppWorld and tau-bench it matches or exceeds GEPA and ACE, with gains of up to 20.7% over GEPA and 21.2% over ACE. Gains are reported as maxima, and the curator adds its own LLM step.
Takeaway: Directly relevant to your prompt-optimization thread: before feeding failed traces to GEPA, check whether judge noise or harness faults, not the prompt, caused them.
