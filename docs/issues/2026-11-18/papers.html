Today's papers quantify the hidden prefix-caching tax of dynamic context editing, show why outcome-only LLM judges miss trajectory failures, and benchmark coding agents as digital employees.

Trending agent papers

arXiv: 3 days ago · 12m
SWE-CoWork: Benchmarking Coding Agents as Digital Employees in Organizations  —  Trending at #5 on Hugging Face Daily Papers (2026-11-17) with 51 upvotes and 4 comments (Tokenwave.AI). Coding agents rarely get tested on ambiguous, multi-person engineering work. SWE-CoWork places agents inside a simulated organization of 26 bounded-knowledge colleagues across 101 assignments in 101 real repositories, requiring requirement gathering and private-test verification. Across 11 evaluated models, the top two pass roughly 63% of fail-to-pass tests yet finish fewer than 8% of full assignments, and 31% of self-reported completed requirements still fail tests. Upfront specs boost test pass rates by ~16 percentage points without raising full completion, showing persistent requirement tracking remains unsolved.
Takeaway: Your coding-agent harness work should track deferred clarification questions and verify claimed requirements against executable checks before closing a task.
arXiv: Nov 9 · 12m
MIRIX-2.0: Multi-Agent Memory System  —  Trending at #4 on Hugging Face Daily Papers (2026-11-16) with 52 upvotes (53 on its paper page), 4 comments, and 3,499 GitHub stars on its public repository. Agent memory systems often lose detail during consolidation or balloon in search cost. MIRIX-2.0 uses six typed memory agents, a provenance hypergraph with budgeted retrieval, and an off-write-path AutoDream module that merges duplicate anchors and distills skills. It reaches 91.4% on LoCoMo and 72.6% on LongMemEval-S (+14.3 points over the strongest baseline), plus 49.2% on MetaClaw-Bench versus 44.0% for MetaClaw. Its benchmarks evaluate multi-agent orchestration overhead mainly via timed QA latency rather than write-path failure modes.
Takeaway: Directly informs your agent memory and context-engineering work by bounding retrieval budgets while preserving exact provenance rows for trace audits.
arXiv: 5 days ago · 10m
Self-Improvement Memory Enables Continual Recursive Self-Improvement  —  Trending at #7 on Hugging Face Daily Papers (2026-11-18) with 30 upvotes and 3 comments (Google). When an agent's base model or benchmark changes, an evolved harness often lacks the history explaining why earlier edits existed. The authors propose Self-Improvement Memory (SIM) for the harness optimizer itself, grouping past edits by recurring failure mode alongside their rationale, predicted versus observed outcomes, and evaluation context. Across four domains, SIM beats harness-only and root-cause-only continuation; across ten independent coding runs, paired continuations raise final scores by an average of 2.36 points (p=0.003). The abstract does not report memory-lookup overhead or public code release.
Takeaway: For your harness and prompt-optimization work, logging rationale plus predicted versus observed deltas prevents regressions when swapping underlying models.
arXiv: 6 days ago · 11m
Actor, Judge, or Teacher? Understanding Decision Models in Long-Horizon Agents  —  Trending on Hugging Face Daily Papers with 38 upvotes, 6 comments, and 49 GitHub stars on its public code (Zhejiang University). It is unclear whether decision models that make weak action choices online can still serve as strong evaluators or RL teachers. Comparing three decision models and three general LLMs on ALFWorld and WebShop, the authors find all six are distracted by easier subgoals when acting, yet accurately credit prerequisite steps when judging full trajectories. Using JEV as a hindsight teacher lifts a Qwen2.5-1.5B GRPO policy from 72.8% to 82.9% on ALFWorld and 56.8% to 71.4% on WebShop. Evaluations are limited to simulated web and household environments.
Takeaway: Relevant to your agent-evaluation and trajectory-scoring work: hindsight step credit yields far cleaner signals than online action selection.
arXiv: Nov 10 · 10m
SteerDistill: Quantizing LLM Agents via Closed-Loop Teacher Steering  —  Trending on Hugging Face Daily Papers with 36 upvotes, 4 comments, and public code (14 GitHub stars). Low-bit quantized agents often miss task constraints mid-rollout, drifting into unrecoverable environment states absent from offline datasets. SteerDistill uses a full-precision teacher in a closed execution loop to steer failed student turns using live environment feedback, then distills those verified corrections into the student for unassisted W4A16 inference. Across five agentic benchmarks and three models, it beats the best quantized baseline by 1.67 to 2.18 average points (staying within 1 point of BF16) and tops QeRL by 1.84 percentage points on tau^2-Bench using 67% fewer GPU hours. Only W4A16 weights are tested.
Takeaway: Worth tracking for serving quantized agents at lower cost without silently breaking multi-step tool compliance.
arXiv: 5 days ago · 12m
WebArena-Pro: A Heterogeneous, Multimodal, Reproducible Benchmark for Web Agents  —  Trending at #13 on Hugging Face Daily Papers (2026-11-16) with 12 upvotes, 3 comments, and public code (McGill NLP Group). Existing reproducible web-agent suites cover few applications and ignore audio and video embedded in pages. WebArena-Pro builds 300 isolated, human-validated tasks across 20 self-hosted web apps in six domain categories with explicit audio and video observation actions. Codex with GPT 6.1 Sol and Claude Code with Claude Opus 5.5 tie at 62.3%, ahead of OpenCode with Kimi 3.1 Pro at 60.7%, while the best GenericAgent setups (Gemini 3.1 Pro omni and Gemma4 31B) reach 30.3%. Even frontier coding-agent harnesses fail nearly 38% of multimodal stateful workflows.
Takeaway: Directly benchmarks the Claude Code, Codex, and OpenCode harnesses you use, revealing where stateful multimodal web tasks still break.

Runtime, harness and production engineering

arXiv: yesterday · 12m
Context Has a Price: Adding and Removing Context for LLM Coding Agents under Prefix Caching  —  Adding retrieved code or pruning old tool outputs is widely assumed to cut coding-agent bills. Analyzing over 20,000 runs of open models on SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench, and Loc-Bench while separating uncached input, cached input, and output tokens, the authors show that context edits often backfire financially. Prepending retrieved evidence cuts read/search commands by 18% to 34% but raises cost by 33% to 42% under a 10x prefix-cache discount without improving resolution. Pruning old observations saves 19% to 36% without caching yet increases cost by 38% to 116% with a 10x cache discount because invalidated prefixes force re-reads and extra steps. Experiments also show concurrent runs can share cached prefixes and skew benchmarks.
Takeaway: Critical for your context-engineering and agent-harness work: evaluate compaction and retrieval policies under real prefix-cache pricing rather than raw token counts.
arXiv: 7 days ago · 14m
The Noise Floor of a Quantization Comparison: Engine, restart, batch and kernel drift, measured against 8-bit and 4-bit effects in two decision models  —  Quantized models are routinely validated against BF16 across different serving engines and batch setups, conflating weight loss with runtime drift. Measuring 2,400 public decisions on Cloudflare's Clef-flash (9B) and Clef (27B) decision models on an NVIDIA GB10 with public code and recipes, the authors establish that non-quantization factors (restarts, engine swaps, batch-size changes, and implicit Gated DeltaNet kernel selection) flip 0% to 0.92% of decisions. Inside one engine, Q8_0 flips 0.88% and 1.04%, FP8 2.12% and 1.62%, and 4-bit formats 3.50% to 5.79%. Aggregate accuracy masks this churn: Clef's Q4_K_M moves net accuracy by -0.02 points while worsening 436 individual decisions. Two pre-registered predictions failed, underscoring how small-N or aggregate checks mislead.
Takeaway: Directly informs your model-serving and quantization validation: isolate engine, batch, and kernel noise floors and audit per-item flips alongside aggregate scores.
arXiv: yesterday · 10m
Readie: Low-Latency Code Sandboxes for AI Agents  —  Offloading agent-generated code to isolated sandboxes improves safety, but cold starts stall iterative loops because a single pre-warmed image cannot stay compact while covering diverse packages and datasets. Readie solves this by learning a small portfolio of compact startup snapshots from training workloads via an approximation algorithm, so each snapshot boots fast while the set covers most scripts. Evaluated across 15,000 public and LLM-synthesized scripts, Readie reduces sandbox startup latency by 3x to 22x and end-to-end runtime by 1.3x to 3.3x under the same storage budget. Coverage relies on workload predictability; novel dependency combinations still fall back outside learned snapshots.
Takeaway: Applicable to your agent-runtime and sandboxing architecture when cold-start latency dominates multi-turn code-execution loops.
arXiv: 2 days ago · 10m
DeltaHarness: Self-Evolving Harnesses for Resource-Adaptive Tool Execution  —  Static tool-scheduling policies fail when hidden runtime conditions invert which execution plan is fastest. DeltaHarness introduces an execution-guided policy search (its QHI loop) that probes cold-start measurements to update runtime dispatch, revise materialization plans, and gate replacements behind an independent confirmation panel. A revised materialization policy cuts complete-delivery latency by 30.0% on a video-editing workflow across three fresh pairs, and on held-out tasks reduces latency by 8.2% over the best fixed plan in one seed and 4.6% over the initial proposal in another. The paper notes latency variance across seeds, leaving open how much probing overhead pays off under rapidly shifting traffic.
Takeaway: Relevant to production agent orchestration where tool latency shifts at runtime and static dispatch plans leave speed on the table.
arXiv: yesterday · 12m
Can You Undo It? Reversibility Classes for AI Agent Actions  —  Granting an agent tool permissions does not specify whether a mistaken call can be rolled back. The authors define Reversibility Classes, a five-level vocabulary with argument-sensitive rules and a prior-state capture contract, mapped across 536 tools in 20 MCP servers. All 738 rule-case checks pass, though native MCP annotations diverge from draft labels on 118 of 481 comparable tools. Across 2,158 recorded runs, the session guard gates all 29 attributable attack-positive runs versus 18 for a read/write proxy, adding nine approval prompts across 1,209 benign runs. Controlled file, Git, SQLite, and browser tests expose a Git merge-state recovery exception and warn that labels alone do not guarantee production recovery.
Takeaway: Directly informs your MCP tool-protocol and guardrail work by gating irreversible side effects with argument-aware state capture.

Evaluation, observability and safety

arXiv: Nov 3 · 12m
trajectory-judge: What Outcome-Only LLM Judges Miss on Agent Trajectories  —  In this NeurIPS 2026 Workshop camera-ready revision (v4 revised 2026-11-03), the author tests why raw fault-injection recall overstates LLM-judge accuracy. Using a deterministic support-desk testbed with 400 exactly labeled trajectories, a 14B outcome-only judge scores 34% to 76% recall on four fault types that leave the final reply unchanged, yet its paired discrimination against clean parent runs is zero. Splitting loud versus silent outcomes yields +0.393 versus +0.048 paired discrimination, whereas prompting the judge to verify each trajectory step reaches +1.00 discrimination on those four fault types with 0 of 100 clean false flags. Even step-wise judging catches only one of four invented-promise types reliably, showing reply-grounding gaps remain.
Takeaway: Essential for your LLM-as-judge and trace-evaluation work: report paired discrimination against clean parents and require step-level checks to avoid illusory recall.
arXiv: Oct 19 · 14m
Understanding When Reflective Prompt and Skill Optimization Helps on Locally Served Reasoning Models: A Two-Model Study of GEPA and SkillOpt  —  Reflective optimizers like GEPA and SkillOpt show strong frontier gains, yet their behavior on locally served reasoning models is underexplored. Evaluating DeepSeek V4 Flash and Qwen3.8-27B on HotpotQA (n=500) and MATH-500 (n=250) under a frozen protocol, the authors find optimization is highly task-dependent. On MATH-500, GEPA lifts exact match from 0.828 to 0.904 on Qwen and 0.852 to 0.908 on DeepSeek while cutting latency, though token consumption rises. On HotpotQA, however, GEPA reverts to the baseline seed on Qwen, neither optimizer beats the baseline, and SkillOpt's validation gains reverse on held-out tasks. Results point to evaluator ambiguity and serving-completion variance as key bottlenecks.
Takeaway: Directly guides your GEPA prompt-optimization work: validate held-out generalization and track token inflation alongside latency when signals are ambiguous.
arXiv: yesterday · 12m
Evolution Under Fire: Evolving Agent Safety Harnesses with Adaptive Injection Stressors  —  Static hand-crafted prompt-injection defenses break as untrusted web and email inputs evolve. Evolution Under Fire pairs utility-constrained, Pareto-scheduled tree search over executable safety harnesses with adaptive stressor evolution that mines utility-preserving attacks from execution traces. On AgentDyn with GPT-4o-mini, evolved harnesses cut attack success from 50.4% to 3.0% while raising both clean and attacked utility; on Agent-SafetyBench with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, attack success drops from 82.6% to 62.0%. Harnesses also transfer zero-shot to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, cutting attack success from 32.1% to 0.9%. Even after evolution, high residual vulnerability on Agent-SafetyBench shows defense-evasion gaps remain.
Takeaway: Connects your Pareto harness-optimization and guardrails threads by co-evolving executable safety checks against adaptive injection stressors.
arXiv: 2 days ago · 10m
ReA-Bench: Benchmarking Responsible Agent Auditing  —  Post-hoc trajectory auditors often miss subtle misconduct or trigger false alarms when aggregate recall hides failure causes. ReA-Bench (v2 revised 2026-11-16) curates realistic production and red-team trajectories into a six-tier diagnostic benchmark covering internal safety knowledge, trajectory contradictions, long-context evidence retrieval, source and reasoning verification, indirect steering, and missing information. It pairs each risky scenario with hard benign controls that look suspicious yet stay authorized, plus shortened and information-complete trajectory variants to isolate whether auditors fail from context length or missing facts. Note that the abstract presents the benchmark's structure without releasing aggregate auditor scores.
Takeaway: Useful for your agent-observability and guardrail evaluations: test false-positive rates on authorized lookalike trajectories and separate context-length failures from evidence gaps.
