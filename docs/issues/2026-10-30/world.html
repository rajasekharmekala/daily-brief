World

CNBC: today · 4m
Iran says four tankers hit mines in Hormuz as fears grow of renewed US strikes after the midterms  —  Iran's Revolutionary Guards said three tankers caught fire after hitting mines on a southern route near Oman and the army said a fourth burned east of the strait, while disputing a US Central Command claim that the main lanes are mine-free. Houthis claimed six attacks on Saudi airports and oil sites. Reports say Washington is weighing renewed action after the Nov. 3 vote while Vice President Vance seeks to reopen talks.
Takeaway: Your Iran and Hormuz tracking: traffic through the strait is down to single-digit daily transits, and the post-midterm window is the next flashpoint.
PBS News (AP): yesterday · 5m
Israel's final election count leaves neither Netanyahu's bloc nor the opposition with a majority  —  Final results give Netanyahu's bloc 53 of 120 Knesset seats, down from 68 at its peak, with the main opposition at 48 and Arab-led parties at 19. Likud called the election "stolen" after the election committee chair asked Shin Bet to look into unverified foreign-interference suspicions; Qatar denied involvement. Results go to the president next Wednesday, who then consults party leaders on who can form a government.
Takeaway: Part of your Middle East watch: who can form Israel's next government matters as US-Iran fighting could resume.
Defense News (Reuters): today · 4m
Pentagon asks Baltic and other NATO allies to tone down public talk about the Russian threat  —  Reuters, citing four people, reports that Pentagon officials including policy chief Elbridge Colby urged Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia to avoid "poking" Russia, as Washington restarts a push for a Ukraine settlement that mixes sanctions relief for Moscow with pressure on Kyiv over strikes on Russian energy sites. Estonia's defence ministry said no such suggestions reached it through official channels; the Pentagon declined to comment on the claims.
Takeaway: Connects your Russia-Ukraine and US-policy interests: it shows how Washington's peace push may sit uneasily with European alarm over sabotage.
Yahoo News: yesterday · 6m
Midterms five days out: Democrats lead the generic ballot by nearly nine points, and the Senate map widens  —  A poll roundup puts Democrats about nine points ahead on the generic ballot, a margin that has held for a month. They need a net three House seats and four Senate seats; the piece says leads are solidifying in Michigan, Georgia and New Hampshire while Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska and South Carolina look increasingly competitive. It cautions that Republican-drawn maps in several Southern states may limit House gains.
Takeaway: Covers your US politics interest: the Nov. 3 result sets the political backdrop for foreign-policy decisions in the coming weeks.
The Japan Times (Reuters): yesterday · 3m
Taiwan says 53 Chinese ships, including carrier Liaoning, are operating from southern Japan to the Philippines  —  Taiwan's National Security Council chief Joseph Wu posted a map of 53 Chinese ships, 34 of them navy warships, spread across waters off southern Japan, Taiwan and the Philippines. Beijing has called the Liaoning's activity routine training. A Taiwanese official said to watch whether the carrier group keeps moving north toward Japan, which would suggest pressure on Japan's defence posture.
Takeaway: Fits your China coverage: a multi-front naval posture touching Japan, Taiwan and the Philippines at once.
Al Jazeera: today · 6m
Ukraine reports record 49,040 Russian casualties in October as it pushes to double drone output  —  Ukraine's General Staff estimates 49,040 Russian dead and incapacitated in October as of Oct. 28, a monthly record above September's 46,230; Al Jazeera notes it cannot verify either side's figures. Kyiv's defence minister asked European allies to fund doubling drone production to about 16 million units next year, and the Netherlands and UK announced new military aid on Oct. 28.
Takeaway: Russia-Ukraine context for your geopolitics reading: the figures are Ukrainian claims, but the drone-mass strategy and European funding are the substantive shifts.

India

The Hindu: today · 6m
ISRO schedules the NVS-03 navigation-satellite launch for 11:51 pm IST on 30 October to help restore NavIC  —  ISRO plans to launch the NVS-03 navigation satellite on a GSLV-F18 from Sriharikota at 11:51 pm IST on 30 October. The Hindu explains that NavIC, India's regional navigation system, has been weakened by repeated on-board atomic-clock failures and by NVS-02 not reaching its intended orbit. A successful NVS-03 would help restore the constellation's services. ISRO describes it as the 20th GSLV flight.
Takeaway: Launch is from Sriharikota in Andhra Pradesh and ties to your space interest; the explainer shows how a satellite programme recovers from earlier failures.
The Hindu: today · 5m
Arnab aftermath: Andhra says power back by Sunday; IMD flags a new system heading for the coast  —  Earlier issues covered Arnab's landfall and the ₹4,600 crore preliminary loss estimate. New on Friday: Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu said home power supply in cyclone-hit areas will be restored by Sunday, 1 November, with farm connections taking four more days. The state reports crops on over 3.5 lakh hectares submerged and about 2,425 km of roads damaged. IMD expects a fresh system to become a depression around 31 October and move towards the Andhra coast.
Takeaway: Covers your Andhra Pradesh follow: the recovery timeline and a second weather system that could reach the coast over the next two days.
The Economic Times: 2 days ago · 6m
Cognizant reported in talks to join Sarvam's round at a $3.5-4 billion valuation  —  The Economic Times, citing people aware of the matter, reports that Cognizant is in talks to take part in Sarvam's new financing, which would value the Bengaluru AI startup at $3.5-4 billion, more than double its previous round. Sarvam is seeking about $300 million. Nothing has been announced; the report ties the interest to questions over how agentic AI will change IT services.
Takeaway: A sign of how Indian IT services firms are buying into foundation-model companies as agents change their business; relevant to your interest in Indian startups and agent platforms.
Telangana Today: 2 days ago · 4m
Telangana bars new borewells for non-drinking use from 1 November as it plans for an El Niño water squeeze  —  From 1 November Telangana will prohibit new borewells for construction and other non-drinking purposes, part of contingency steps for an expected El Niño-driven water crisis. Hyderabad builders fear higher costs and delays, and rig operators and daily-wage workers fear lost income. The Chief Secretary has asked for micro-level plans to avoid drinking-water and power shortages, and offices have been told to cut electricity use.
Takeaway: Follows Telangana and Hyderabad: it shows how the state's drought and water planning is starting to affect the city's construction and daily life.
The Hindu BusinessLine (PTI): 2 days ago · 3m
Cabinet approves ₹1,856.67 crore scheme for sports goods manufacturing zones  —  On 28 October the Union Cabinet approved the Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme, with an outlay of ₹1,856.67 crore for 2026 to 2033-34. It plans dedicated Sports Goods Manufacturing Zones that co-locate manufacturers, suppliers, testing facilities and design centres, linked to existing BHAVYA scheme and NICDC industrial parks. It also covers research, skills, quality certification and market development.
Takeaway: A concrete India sports-policy move, aimed at making equipment domestically; broad interest rather than tied to one of your threads.
Prime Minister of India (PIB release): 2 days ago · 3m
India approves ₹10,000 crore programme to build a domestic shipping-container industry  —  The Cabinet cleared the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme, ₹10,000 crore through FY 2039-40, to cut import dependence and strengthen supply chains. Of that, ₹859 crore funds 3% interest subvention on term loans, ₹8,891 crore operational support per container sold, and ₹150 crore testing and certification. The government expects about 3,000 direct and 50,000 indirect jobs. Reuters notes the move comes as conflicts disrupt shipping routes.
Takeaway: Industrial policy and trade-resilience story from your India economy follow, with a supply-chain angle.
