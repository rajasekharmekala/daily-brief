Beerakaya Guddu Vepudu (Ridge Gourd and Egg Fry)బీరకాయ గుడ్డు వేపుడు

Tender ridge gourd is sweated in its own juices with a curry-leaf talimpu, then four eggs are folded through until they set in soft, spicy curds that hold the sweet, juicy gourd. It is a classic Andhra pairing, made in one pan in under twenty minutes.

6.1g net carbs  ·  14g protein  ·  17g fat  ·  243 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 65% ● Protein 24% ● Net Carbs 10%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
బీరకాయ ఇలా గుడ్డుతో ట్రై చేయండి #beerakaya #egg #curry #tastyfood #tasty #homefood — AndhraParis Madhu vlogs
Ingredients
  • ridge gourd (beerakaya), peeled and cut into 1 cm cubes about 1 small gourd · 120g
  • eggs, shelled and beaten with a pinch of salt 4 large · 200g
  • ghee 1 tbsp · 14g
  • mustard seeds 1/3 tsp · 1g
  • cumin seeds 1/2 tsp · 1g
  • urad dal, for crunch in the tempering 1/2 tsp · 2g
  • dried red chilli, torn 2 chillies · 2g
  • fresh curry leaves about 10 leaves · 1.5g
  • onion, finely chopped about 1.5 tbsp · 20g
  • garlic, minced 2 small cloves · 5g
  • green chilli, slit 2 chillies · 8g
  • turmeric powder 1/4 tsp · 0.8g
  • Guntur red chilli powder 3/4 tsp · 2g
  • coriander leaves, chopped a small handful · 6g
  • salt to taste · 3g
Method
  1. Peel the ridge gourd completely, because leftover ridges stay tough, and snip off both ends. Taste a raw sliver of one end; if it is bitter, discard that gourd. Cut into 1 cm cubes.
  2. Heat the ghee in a wide kadai over medium-high until it shimmers. Add the mustard seeds and wait for the spluttering to stop (about 20 seconds), then add the cumin, urad dal and torn dried chillies and fry 20 seconds until the dal turns pale gold.
  3. Add the curry leaves (stand back, they spit), garlic and green chilli and fry 30 seconds until the curry leaves stop spluttering and the garlic smells nutty, not brown.
  4. Add the onion and cook 2 minutes until translucent with golden edges. Add the ridge gourd, turmeric and half the salt. Do not add water: the gourd releases plenty. Stir, cover and cook on medium-low for 5 to 6 minutes, stirring once, until the cubes are soft but still hold their shape.
  5. Uncover, turn the heat to medium-high and cook 1 minute to boil off most of the liquid. Stir in the chilli powder now, not earlier, so it does not scorch; cook 20 seconds.
  6. Pour in the beaten eggs and the remaining salt. Leave for 15 seconds, then fold and scrape every 10 seconds for about 2 minutes until the eggs form moist golden curds. Switch off while still glossy, since carry-over heat finishes them.
  7. Fold through the coriander leaves and serve at once. Make-ahead: the cooked gourd base (step 5) keeps 2 days in the fridge; reheat and add the eggs fresh.
🌉 US & Bay Area Ingredient Adaptations
  • Fresh ridge gourd (beerakaya / turai): Zucchini or Chinese okra (also called silk squash), peeled if the skin is thick; salt and drain zucchini 5 minutes first because it is wetter
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers (Santa Clara) or New India Bazaar (Sunnyvale) for ridge gourd; 99 Ranch for Chinese silk squash; any Safeway or Trader Joe's for zucchini
  • Fresh curry leaves: Frozen curry leaves; dried leaves are a weak last resort
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers (Santa Clara) or New India Bazaar (Sunnyvale), freezer aisle
  • Guntur red chilli powder: Cayenne with a little paprika for colour
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Costco or any Safeway spice aisle
Make it taste like home
  • Taste a raw sliver of the gourd before cooking; a bitter one will ruin the dish, and ridge gourd is bitter now and then.
  • Keto: the gourd supplies most of the carbs here, so weigh it; extra onion adds carbs quickly.
  • Heat dial 4: drop the chilli powder and the dried chillies to bring it to a 2; add a pinch of black pepper instead.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • Use zucchini in place of the ridge gourd, salted and squeezed lightly.
  • Add 1/2 tsp ginger paste for a sharper edge.

Kothimeera Kodi (Andhra Green Coriander Chicken)కొత్తిమీర కోడి

In Andhra homes coriander is the base of the masala, not a garnish. A fiery paste of fresh coriander, green chilli, ginger, garlic and a little coconut coats seared chicken thigh for a vivid green, herb-sharp curry that stays dry enough to scoop with cauliflower rice.

7.2g net carbs  ·  42g protein  ·  26g fat  ·  448 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 55% ● Protein 39% ● Net Carbs 7%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
అమోఘమైన రుచితో కొత్తిమీర కోడి కూర | Kothimeera Kodi Kura | Chicken Curry Recipes — Vismai Food
Ingredients
  • boneless skinless chicken thigh, 2.5 cm pieces about 4 thighs · 400g
  • fresh coriander leaves and tender stems, for the paste about 2 packed cups · 60g
  • green chillies, for the paste 4 to 5 chillies · 20g
  • ginger, for the paste 2 cm piece · 10g
  • garlic, for the paste 4 cloves · 10g
  • unsweetened desiccated coconut, for the paste 2 tbsp · 12g
  • sesame oil (nuvvula noone) 1 1/2 tbsp · 20g
  • ghee, to finish 1/2 tbsp · 7g
  • cumin seeds 1/2 tsp · 1.5g
  • dried red chilli 2 chillies · 2g
  • fresh curry leaves about 12 leaves · 2g
  • onion, finely chopped about 3 tbsp · 35g
  • coriander seed powder 1 1/2 tsp · 3g
  • garam masala 3/4 tsp · 2g
  • black pepper, freshly ground 1/2 tsp · 1g
  • turmeric powder 1/4 tsp · 1g
  • lemon juice 2 tsp · 10g
  • salt to taste · 4g
Method
  1. Blend the coriander, green chillies, ginger, garlic and coconut with 3 tbsp of water to a thick, slightly coarse paste; scrape down once. Keep this bright green and do not over-blend.
  2. Toss the chicken with the turmeric, 1/2 tsp of the salt and the black pepper and set aside while you start the pan (a few minutes is enough, the pieces are small).
  3. Heat the sesame oil in the kadai over medium-high. Add the cumin and torn dried chillies and fry 15 seconds until the cumin darkens and smells toasty. Add the curry leaves and wait until they stop spluttering.
  4. Add the onion and cook 4 minutes, stirring often, until deep golden at the edges; a pinch of salt speeds this up. Add the chicken in one layer and sear 3 minutes, turning once, until the surface is opaque and lightly browned.
  5. Stir in the coriander powder and garam masala for 20 seconds, then add the green paste. Cook 4 to 5 minutes on medium, stirring, until the raw smell is gone and the oil begins to separate at the edges.
  6. Add 60 ml water, cover and cook on medium-low for 6 minutes until the chicken is cooked through and the sauce clings; uncover for the last minute to reduce if it is loose.
  7. Finish with the ghee, lemon juice and remaining salt. Rest 3 minutes. The green stays brightest if you do not simmer it hard after the paste goes in. Make-ahead: the paste keeps 2 days in the fridge, or freeze in an ice-cube tray.
🌉 US & Bay Area Ingredient Adaptations
  • Fresh coriander (kothimeera) in quantity: Ordinary cilantro bunches, using the tender stems too
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Any Safeway, Trader Joe's or Costco; 99 Ranch and Indian grocers sell large cheap bunches
  • Fresh curry leaves: Frozen curry leaves
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers (Santa Clara) or New India Bazaar (Sunnyvale), freezer aisle
  • Chicken, natu kodi style: Boneless thigh (as written); bone-in thigh chopped small from a halal market also works with 10 more minutes
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Costco, or a halal market in Santa Clara or Sunnyvale
Make it taste like home
  • Taste tip: blend the stems in as well; they carry most of the coriander flavour.
  • Keto: fresh coriander is very low in net carbs, which is why this much herb costs almost nothing; the onion and garlic are the real carbs, so weigh them.
  • Heat dial 4: use two green chillies for a 3; for 5, add 1 tsp Guntur chilli powder with the coriander powder.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • Use chicken breast cut small and shorten the covered step to 4 minutes.
  • Replace the coconut with 6 g almond flour if you have none.

Vankaya Mamsam Keema Kura (Brinjal and Goat Mince Curry)వంకాయ మాంసం కీమా కూర

Goat mince browned deep in sesame oil, a talimpu of cumin and curry leaves, and cubes of small brinjal that turn silky and soak up the masala. It is mutton-and-vankaya, an Andhra favourite, shortened with a pressure cooker to a weeknight half hour.

8.8g net carbs  ·  39g protein  ·  23g fat  ·  411 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 52% ● Protein 39% ● Net Carbs 9%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
Vankaya Nuvvula Kura Recipe !! — Vismai Food
Ingredients
  • goat mince (or ground lamb) about 3/4 lb · 350g
  • small brinjal, 2 cm cubes 2 small Indian brinjals · 120g
  • sesame oil (nuvvula noone) 1 1/2 tbsp · 20g
  • ghee, to finish 1/2 tbsp · 7g
  • cumin seeds 1/2 tsp · 1.5g
  • dried red chilli 2 chillies · 2g
  • fresh curry leaves about 12 leaves · 2g
  • onion, finely chopped about 3 tbsp · 40g
  • ginger, minced 2 tsp · 8g
  • garlic, minced 3 cloves · 8g
  • green chilli, slit 2 chillies · 10g
  • tomato, chopped half a small tomato · 50g
  • Guntur red chilli powder 1 1/2 tsp · 4g
  • coriander seed powder 1 1/2 tsp · 3g
  • turmeric powder 1/4 tsp · 1g
  • garam masala 3/4 tsp · 2g
  • unsweetened desiccated coconut 2 tbsp · 12g
  • coriander leaves, chopped a handful · 8g
  • salt to taste · 4g
Method
  1. Heat the sesame oil in the open pressure cooker over medium-high. Fry the cumin and torn dried chillies for 15 seconds, then add the curry leaves and wait until they stop spluttering.
  2. Add the onion and cook 4 minutes until golden at the edges. Add ginger, garlic and green chilli and fry 1 minute until the raw smell goes.
  3. Add the goat mince, breaking it up, with half the salt. Cook on high 5 minutes, stirring, until the water it releases has boiled off and the meat browns and sizzles; this browning is the base of the flavour.
  4. Stir in the turmeric, coriander powder and tomato and cook 2 minutes until the tomato collapses. Add the chilli powder and garam masala for 20 seconds only so they do not burn.
  5. Add the brinjal cubes, the remaining salt and 80 ml water. Close the lid and cook for 2 whistles (about 6 minutes on medium-high), then let the pressure drop naturally for 3 minutes. Without a pressure cooker, cover and simmer 20 minutes, adding 30 ml more water if it catches.
  6. Uncover, stir in the desiccated coconut and simmer on medium 3 minutes until the brinjal is silky and the masala clings to the mince. Finish with the ghee and coriander. Make-ahead: tastes better the next day; keeps 3 days refrigerated.
🌉 US & Bay Area Ingredient Adaptations
  • Goat mince (mamsam keema): Ground lamb, 85 percent lean, which is easiest to find; or mince boneless goat shoulder in a food processor
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Halal markets in Santa Clara and Sunnyvale for ground goat; Costco or Safeway for ground lamb
  • Small Indian brinjal (vankaya): Chinese or Japanese long eggplant, 2 cm cubes; if only a globe eggplant is available, salt it for 10 minutes and sear the cubes first
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers (Santa Clara), 99 Ranch, or any Trader Joe's
  • Fresh curry leaves: Frozen curry leaves
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers (Santa Clara) or New India Bazaar (Sunnyvale), freezer aisle
Make it taste like home
  • Taste tip: do not rush the mince browning in step 3; patience gives the roasted note that makes this taste like home cooking.
  • Keto: the brinjal is weighed at 60 g per serving; going to 200 g adds about 3 g net carbs per serving.
  • Heat dial 4: use 2 tsp chilli powder for a 3; for a 5, add 2 more green chillies.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • Use chicken thigh mince in place of goat for a 20-minute version.
  • Add 1/2 tsp black pepper at the end for a pepper keema finish.

Keto science and tips

Nutrition (journal) via PubMed: Aug 1 · 4m
Systematic review: plant-based low-carb and keto diets in 7 trials, strong short-term effects but adherence slips outside controlled settings  —  University of Pavia and Milan authors reviewed seven randomised trials (976 adults, 4 to 52 weeks) of predominantly plant-based low-carbohydrate and ketogenic diets. Both versions cut body weight by 5 to 7 kg and lowered LDL-cholesterol and HbA1c, with moderate GRADE certainty for weight and lipids. Adherence was high in controlled settings but declined in free-living conditions; plant-based low-carb looked easier to sustain than plant-based keto. Sustainability evidence was very low.
Takeaway: Directly on your keto sustainability and adherence interest, and relevant to vegetable- and paneer-heavy Andhra-style menus: it separates short-term effect from long-term feasibility.
Obesity (journal) via PubMed: Oct 6 · 4m
8-week trial: a very-low-carbohydrate diet cut thigh muscle fat 24% versus 1% on a low-fat diet in older adults, with better grip and knee strength gains tied to it  —  University of Alabama at Birmingham researchers ran a secondary analysis of a randomised trial in adults aged 60 or older. Over 8 weeks of weight-maintaining diets, a very-low-carbohydrate diet (under 10% of energy from carbohydrate) lowered intermuscular adipose tissue measured by MRI by 24.4%, versus 1.0% on a low-fat diet. Larger falls in that fat were linked to better grip strength, knee extension and reach scores. It is a secondary analysis in a specific age group, with a short duration.
Takeaway: Speaks to your protein and muscle-quality questions on keto: a human trial where muscle function was measured, not only weight, though short and in a different population.
Frontiers in Nutrition: Sep 21 · 6m
Mini review: brief, repeated ketone rises may be a normal part of brain energy flexibility, not just a rescue fuel in disease  —  A Mini Review by Antonio Paoli argues that most ketone-and-cognition research has focused on Alzheimer's and mild cognitive impairment, where glucose use by the brain is impaired. It proposes a broader hypothesis: short, repeated periods of mild physiological ketosis, from fasting, exercise, carbohydrate restriction or MCT intake, may support long-term brain health through mitochondrial, antioxidant and BDNF-related pathways. It is a hypothesis paper; the author calls for studies that measure the frequency and size of ketone rises.
Takeaway: Adds mechanism to your keto-science reading: it frames ketosis as a switch between fuels rather than a permanent state, which bears on how strict versus intermittent approaches are discussed.
The Journal of Physiology via PubMed: Sep 27 · 4m
Crossover study of 10 trained men: exercise plus a low-carbohydrate diet raised peak fat burning during exercise without changing mitochondrial efficiency  —  Danish researchers at the University of Copenhagen tested how muscle glycogen affects fat use in ten trained men. After an exercise session that depleted glycogen, a low-carbohydrate day raised peak fat oxidation and the intensity at which it peaks (Fat max) more than a high-carbohydrate day. Mitochondrial coupling control and efficiency did not change, so the shift was not explained by mitochondrial function. The sample is small and limited to trained men over about a day.
Takeaway: Useful physiology behind keto adaptation: it separates the effect of low fuel availability on fat use from mitochondrial changes, a thread that links to your earlier mitochondria and ketosis reading.
