Cosmos and Life

ESO and Hubble/Webb lead the sky: a double debris disc around a white dwarf, the Cat's Head Nebula, NASA's UVEX confirmation and a reionization simulation. In biology, the first cultured Heimdallarchaeia give a new look at the archaeal kin of eukaryotes.

Space, astronomy and cosmology

ESO: 3 days ago · 4m
VLT finds a double debris disc around a white dwarf, shaped by two surviving planetary bodies  —  Using X-Shooter on ESO's Very Large Telescope, astronomers found that the white dwarf J1328−1450 is ringed by two concentric gas discs separated by a gap, unlike the single flat disc seen around every other known case. The team infers two surviving planetesimals, one a few kilometres across and one possibly Pluto- or Moon-sized, and finds different magnesium content in the two rings. The study appears in Nature Astronomy.
Takeaway: A concrete look at how a planetary system ends, in the exoplanet and stellar-remnant territory you follow under your space and cosmology interest.
ESA/Webb: yesterday · 3m
Hubble and Webb image the Cat's Head Nebula in the Carina Nebula  —  ESA, NASA and STScI released paired Hubble visible-light and Webb near- and mid-infrared views of a dark dust pillar shaped like a seated cat, about 7,500 light-years away in the Carina Nebula. Hubble shows an opaque pillar with a possible embedded young star as its glowing eye; Webb sees through more of the dust to reveal many stars and faint background galaxies.
Takeaway: A Webb-plus-Hubble image set, the kind of stunning-image story your space interests ask for, released just ahead of Halloween.
NASA Science: 3 days ago · 3m
NASA confirms UVEX ultraviolet sky-survey telescope for launch by January 2031  —  After passing its Key Decision Point C review, NASA's UVEX mission is committed to launch by January 2031 with a total lifecycle cost of $590 million. It will survey the whole sky in ultraviolet light and complements wide surveys such as the Roman Space Telescope. It can also repoint quickly to catch the explosions that follow gravitational-wave bursts from merging neutron stars.
Takeaway: Links wide-field sky surveys with follow-up of gravitational-wave events, which fits your LIGO interest and today's black-hole and gravitational-wave deep dive.
MIT News: 4 days ago · 5m
Lumina simulation follows hydrogen and helium reionization in the first 2 billion years  —  Lumina, described in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, is a radiation-hydrodynamics simulation of the early universe from the dark ages through roughly the first 2 billion years. MIT reports it covers a volume about 100 times larger than previous best simulations and is the first to capture both hydrogen and helium reionization, with supermassive black holes driving the helium phase.
Takeaway: Early-universe structure and black-hole growth are core to your cosmology interest, and JWST's early-galaxy finds are what such simulations are built to test.
University of Arizona News: 2 days ago · 4m
Hubble sees the 'Cat's Tail' in Beta Pictoris in visible light, pointing to a collision of dwarf-planet-size bodies  —  Hubble's STIS coronagraph has detected, for the first time in visible light, the Cat's Tail feature in the Beta Pictoris debris disk, about 63 light-years away. The tail is mostly dark carbon-rich organic material and likely stretches up to 1,000 astronomical units. The team reads it as the aftermath of a collision of Pluto-like bodies, and also reports a warp in the dust halo likely tied to the gas giant Beta Pic b.
Takeaway: A direct view of how young planetary systems get shaped, combining Hubble with earlier JWST data, in line with your exoplanet interest.

Biology and science

Phys.org (Kyoto University release on a Nature paper): yesterday · 4m
First cultured Heimdallarchaeia, close archaeal kin of eukaryotes, were simple inside but covered in branching protrusions  —  A JAMSTEC and Kyoto University team isolated and grew two strains of Heimdallarchaeia, an archaeal group thought to be the closest relatives of eukaryotes. Neither strain had a nucleus or other membrane-bound organelles, but both grew long branching surface protrusions used to partner with other microbes; one tolerated small amounts of oxygen when aerobic bacteria were present. The authors read this as support for surface-based symbiosis preceding the mitochondrial partnership.
Takeaway: Directly on your origin-of-eukaryotes and bioenergetics interest: it tests how the host cell and the mitochondrial ancestor first got together.
Broad Institute (describing papers in Cell): Oct 1 · 5m
Mitochondrial proteomes across the eukaryote tree point to a relatively late mitochondrial origin  —  Researchers built mitochondrial protein inventories for diverse eukaryotes, including parasites, then used the data to retrain a machine-learning predictor for hundreds of untested species. Their reconstruction of when organelles first appeared supports mitochondria arriving relatively late, after the ancestral eukaryote had already developed other complex parts. The data also flag mitochondrial proteins shared by several pathogens but absent in humans, which the authors suggest as drug-target candidates.
Takeaway: Pairs with today's archaeal-ancestor item: it is the comparative-data side of the early-versus-late mitochondria debate in eukaryote origins.
Nature Portfolio press release (Nature paper, 14 Oct 2026): Oct 15 · 3m
Yuezhuornis, a Jurassic bird from China, appears to have been flightless and ground-living  —  Min Wang and colleagues describe Yuezhuornis yangyuanensis, a roughly 148 to 150 million-year-old bird from Fujian, estimated at about 821 grams with forelimbs about one-third shorter than hind limbs. Comparison with 377 living bird species put its limb proportions closest to ground-dwelling birds such as pheasants. The authors infer that earliest birds were ecologically more varied than assumed, while noting it rests on one incomplete specimen.
Takeaway: Adds an evolution item on early bird diversity; the single-specimen caveat is worth keeping in mind.
