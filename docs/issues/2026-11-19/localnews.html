San Jose and the South Bay

PR Newswire: today · 3m
San Jose Approves Verra Mobility Contract for 33-Location Speed Camera Pilot  —  The City of San José approved a contract with Verra Mobility to deploy, operate, and maintain automated speed safety cameras at 33 high-risk corridors, beginning with an initial six sites. San José is the sixth and final California city authorized under Assembly Bill 645, following San Francisco and Oakland, which reported 79% and 70% speeding reductions. Starting as early as December 14, drivers traveling 11 mph or more over the posted limit will receive warnings during a mandatory warning period before civil fines take effect without driver-license points.
Takeaway: Directly affects San Jose drivers and road safety starting December 14 across 33 high-injury corridors.
SFGATE: today · 4m
Santa Clara County to Merge TRUST Mobile Crisis Line Into 988 Hotline  —  Facing county budget pressures, Santa Clara County will fold its Trusted Response Urgent Support Team (TRUST) hotline into the 988 suicide and crisis line starting February 2027, with full 988 dispatcher takeover in March. County data presented to supervisors showed 988 answered 95% of over 37,500 calls in fiscal year 2025–26, while TRUST answered 74% of 15,540 calls and redirected 14% to 988. Behavioral Health Services is filling six roles and requesting four dispatchers as community advocates press to preserve non-police mobile crisis routing.
Takeaway: Tracks a major Santa Clara County budget and public-safety service consolidation taking effect in early 2027.
San Jose Inside: today · 4m
Federal Grand Jury Indicts Alleged Russian Agent Over Palo Alto Tech Surveillance  —  Northern District of California U.S. Attorney Craig H. Missakian announced a federal grand jury indictment charging 25-year-old Russian national Igor D'iu—arrested October 30 in East Palo Alto—with acting as an unregistered foreign agent, interstate stalking, and identity document fraud. Federal filings allege D'iu gathered intelligence for Russia by surveilling employees of a Silicon Valley defense-technology company with a major Palo Alto office, identified by Bloomberg and BBC as Palantir Technologies, because of its support for Ukraine.
Takeaway: Highlights active counterintelligence and physical-security risks inside the South Bay technology corridor.
ArcaMax Publishing: today · 4m
San Jose Council Weighs Making Cost-of-Living Affordability an Official Focus Area  —  At a special San Jose City Council study session this week, staff reported residents face the highest essential household bills among the 50 largest US cities, averaging nearly $45,000 annually as child care averages $3,650 a month and rent plus utilities $3,132. Individuals earning below $113,700 qualify as low income in Santa Clara County, while median November one-bedroom rents hit $2,738. Councilmembers are divided ahead of an early-2027 decision on whether to elevate affordability into a formal budget focus area alongside housing production and public safety.
Takeaway: Outlines San Jose's upcoming 2027 budget priorities on housing supply, developer fee deferrals, and PG&E rate pressure.
La Voz News: today · 4m
First Major Winter Storm Strands VTA Bus and Tests San Jose Flood Hotspots  —  Wednesday's November 18 downpour triggered National Weather Service flood advisories across San Jose, Cupertino, Sunnyvale, Santa Clara, Mountain View, and Milpitas, testing South Bay drainage infrastructure. A VTA Route 23 bus struck a submerged concrete bike-lane separator, requiring two fire trucks and an ambulance, while San Jose firefighters rescued two motorists from deep water near West Santa Clara Street and Stockton Avenue by SAP Center. City emergency officials said public works crews are clearing 35,000 storm drains and monitoring over 60 flood hotspots.
Takeaway: Pinpoints South Bay transit disruptions and city flood-monitoring hotspots ahead of winter El Niño storms.

Bay Area and California

KQED: yesterday · 4m
SFMTA Approves $55M Hayden AI Bus-Camera Contract to Enforce Transit Lanes  —  On November 17, San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency directors approved a $55 million three-year contract with San Francisco-based Hayden AI to install automated enforcement cameras on 212 Muni vehicles. Currently, parking control officers manually review thousands of hours of bus video to issue 6,000 to 8,000 citations annually for illegal transit-lane and bus-stop parking. The upgraded AI cameras automatically flag violations and transmit digital evidence packages, which SFMTA estimates could let staff process up to five times as many citations and improve wheelchair ramp boarding.
Takeaway: Blends Bay Area transit policy with production computer-vision enforcement deployment on public fleets.
San Jose Inside: 2 days ago · 4m
Thirty California District Attorneys Petition Supreme Court to Halt 548 Commutations  —  District attorneys from 30 California counties—including San Mateo County DA Stephen M. Wagstaffe, though not Santa Clara County DA Jeff Rosen—filed suit at the California Supreme Court to block Governor Gavin Newsom's proposed blanket commutations of 548 death row sentences. Prosecutors argue Newsom bypassed statutory requirements to notify victims' families and give them an opportunity to be heard, while Newsom contends that rule applies only to prisoner-initiated applications. The high court must also review nearly 400 cases involving multiple felony convictions under state law.
Takeaway: Covers a consequential California Supreme Court constitutional test over executive clemency powers and victims' statutory rights.
Local News Matters: yesterday · 3m
San Mateo County Supervisors Enact Immediate 45-Day Data Center Moratorium  —  The San Mateo County Board of Supervisors unanimously passed an immediate 45-day urgency ordinance banning the siting, construction, conversion, or expansion of data centers across all unincorporated zoning districts. Introduced by Supervisor Lisa Gauthier after developers solicited the San Mateo County Event Center as a potential site, the pause gives staff time to draft zoning standards for AI facilities' heavy water and electricity demands. Before the 45-day window expires, supervisors may extend the moratorium by one year and renew it for a second year.
Takeaway: Shows Peninsula counties joining South Bay jurisdictions in tightening zoning scrutiny over AI data center power and water loads.
KQED: today · 6m
Investigation Finds California Left Educators Accused of Abuse in Classrooms  —  A joint KQED and ProPublica investigation published November 19 found California's teacher licensing system allowed at least three educators reported for alleged sexual misconduct to keep their credentials and take jobs at new schools until criminal charges were filed years later. In one Bay Area case, an elementary teacher acquitted in 2007 received a confidential state warning expunged after three years, enabling him to keep teaching before drawing new complaints in 2022–23. Because unpunished reports carry no public database flag, hiring districts remained unaware.
Takeaway: Exposes structural oversight gaps in California's Commission on Teacher Credentialing and public school hiring checks.
Local News Matters: today · 3m
San Francisco Moves to Merge Planning, Building Inspection, and Permit Center  —  San Francisco Mayor Daniel Lurie announced plans Wednesday to merge the Planning Department, Department of Building Inspection, and Permit Center into a single Department of Planning and Building under charter authority granted by voter-approved Proposition F, which passed with about 59% support. Expected to take effect 60 days after post-certification legislation is introduced in December, the reorganization unifies design review, permitting, and inspections under one leadership team while retaining oversight from the Planning, Building Inspection, and Historic Preservation commissions.
Takeaway: Tracks a major structural permitting reform intended to accelerate Bay Area housing construction and small-business openings.
