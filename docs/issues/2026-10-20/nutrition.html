Pachi Mirapa Guddu Vepudu (Andhra Green-Chilli Egg Fry)పచ్చిమిర్చి గుడ్డు వేపుడు

Boiled eggs blistered golden in groundnut oil, then tossed in a fiery coriander and green-chilli masala that clings like a dry curry. It is the green-chilli cousin of the usual red karam egg fry: sharper, fresher and ready in about 20 minutes.

5.4g net carbs  ·  20g protein  ·  26g fat  ·  340 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 70% ● Protein 24% ● Net Carbs 6%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
బ్యాచిలర్స్ స్పెషల్ టమాటో గుడ్డు పులుసు Easy Tomato Egg Drop Curry | Egg pulsu recipe @VismaiFood — Vismai Food
Ingredients
  • large eggs, hard-boiled, peeled and halved lengthwise 6 eggs · 300g
  • groundnut oil 1 tbsp · 14g
  • ghee 1 tsp · 5g
  • green chillies, 18 g blended and 4 g slit about 6 Indian green chillies · 22g
  • fresh coriander leaves and tender stems 1 small bunch · 20g
  • onion, very finely chopped 2 tbsp · 20g
  • garlic cloves 1 large clove · 5g
  • ginger 1 cm piece · 4g
  • curry leaves 1 sprig · 2g
  • mustard seeds 1/3 tsp · 1g
  • cumin seeds 1/3 tsp · 1g
  • turmeric powder a pinch · 0.5g
  • black pepper, freshly crushed about 1/2 tsp · 1g
  • lemon juice 1 tsp · 5g
  • salt about 1/3 tsp · 2g
Method
  1. Lower the eggs into a pan of boiling water and cook 9 minutes. Move them straight into iced water for 3 minutes, peel, halve lengthwise and pat very dry. Dry surfaces blister; wet ones steam. Prick each yolk side once or twice with a fork so the masala can cling.
  2. While the eggs cook, blend 18 g of the green chillies, the coriander, garlic, ginger and 1 tablespoon of water into a coarse, bright-green paste. Slit the remaining 4 g chilli lengthwise.
  3. Heat the groundnut oil in the skillet over medium-high heat. Add the curry leaves and slit chilli; they are ready when the leaves stop spluttering and turn glassy, about 30 seconds. Scoop them out and keep them for garnish.
  4. Lay the eggs in the pan cut-side down, sprinkle with the turmeric and a third of the salt, and leave them alone for 60 to 90 seconds until the whites are blistered and golden at the edges. Flip for 20 seconds, then lift the eggs onto a plate.
  5. Drop the heat to medium. Add the ghee, mustard seeds and cumin; when the mustard stops popping (about 20 seconds) add the onion and cook 2 minutes until translucent with golden edges.
  6. Tip in the green paste and the remaining salt. Fry 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until the raw smell is gone, the colour dulls from bright to olive and a few drops of oil show at the edge of the pan. Keep the heat moderate so the green does not scorch and turn bitter.
  7. Return the eggs to the pan and fold gently for 1 to 2 minutes on medium-low until every piece is coated and the masala looks dry and clingy. Take off the heat, add the pepper and lemon juice, scatter the crisp curry leaves and chilli on top, and let it sit 2 minutes before serving.
Make it taste like home
  • Heat control: heat 4 as written. For heat 2, use 3 green chillies and de-seed them; for heat 5, add a spoon of finely chopped Thai bird chillies to the paste.
  • Keto: the paste carries the flavour, so you need only 20 g of onion for the whole pan. Most of the net carbs come from the green-chilli and aromatic paste; the eggs add under 2 g per serving.
  • Make ahead: boil and peel the eggs and blend the green paste up to 2 days ahead (paste in a jar under a film of oil); keep both refrigerated, covered; then breakfast is a 10-minute skillet job. Eat the same day; reheated eggs turn rubbery.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • Quail eggs (12 for 6 hen eggs) make a good party version.
  • Use all groundnut oil in place of ghee for a dairy-free dish.

Gongura Royyala Kura (Sorrel Prawn Masala)గోంగూర రొయ్యల కూర

Prawns seared hot, then folded into a thick olive-dark gongura masala and finished with a sizzling garlic and red-chilli talimpu. The sourness comes entirely from the leaf, so there is no tamarind and no jaggery.

8.7g net carbs  ·  23g protein  ·  17g fat  ·  289 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 55% ● Protein 32% ● Net Carbs 12%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
గోదారొళ్ల స్పెషల్ గోంగూర రొయ్యలు కర్రీ | Gongura Prawns Curry Recipe in Telugu — Vismai Food
Ingredients
  • medium prawns, peeled and deveined (thawed if frozen) about 24 prawns · 300g
  • gongura (sorrel) leaves, picked from stems, washed and fully dried, chopped about 3 packed cups · 120g
  • groundnut oil 1 1/2 tbsp · 22g
  • sesame oil, for the final talimpu 1 1/2 tsp · 6g
  • onion, finely chopped a quarter of a medium onion · 30g
  • green chillies, slit 3 chillies · 12g
  • garlic, 4 g crushed with the ginger and 3 g sliced for the talimpu 2 large cloves · 7g
  • ginger 1 cm piece · 4g
  • Guntur karam (red chilli powder) 1 1/2 tsp · 4.5g
  • dried red chillies for the talimpu 2 whole · 1.5g
  • coriander powder 1 tsp · 3g
  • turmeric powder 1/3 tsp · 0.8g
  • curry leaves 1 sprig · 2g
  • cumin seeds 1/3 tsp · 1g
  • mustard seeds 1/3 tsp · 1g
  • cinnamon stick, a 1 cm piece 0.4g
  • green cardamom pods, lightly crushed 2 pods · 0.5g
  • salt about 1/2 tsp · 3g
Method
  1. Pat the prawns completely dry. Toss with the turmeric, a third of the salt and half of the Guntur karam; set aside while you prepare the gongura.
  2. Strip the gongura leaves from the stems, wash, and spin or pat them very dry (wet leaves stew instead of frying and dilute the sourness). Chop coarsely.
  3. Heat 1 tablespoon of the groundnut oil in a kadai over high heat until it shimmers. Sear the prawns in two batches, 45 to 60 seconds per side, just until they curl and turn pink on the outside. They will finish cooking later. Tip them onto a plate.
  4. Lower the heat to medium. Add the remaining groundnut oil, the cumin, mustard seeds, cinnamon and cardamom. When the mustard stops popping (about 20 seconds) add the onion, the slit green chillies and the curry leaves; cook 3 minutes until the onion is soft with golden edges.
  5. Stir in the crushed garlic and ginger for 45 seconds, then the remaining Guntur karam and the coriander powder with a splash of water so the spices do not burn. Add all the gongura and a pinch of salt. Cook 4 to 5 minutes, mashing with the back of a spoon, until the leaves collapse into a dark olive pulp and the oil starts to ooze back out at the edges.
  6. Add 3 tablespoons of water, scrape the pan and simmer 1 minute. Return the prawns and their juices, toss to coat, cover and cook on medium-low for 2 to 3 minutes, until the prawns are opaque and firm. Do not go longer or they turn rubbery.
  7. For the talimpu, heat the sesame oil in a small pan over medium heat. Add the dried red chillies and sliced garlic; when the garlic turns golden (about 30 seconds) pour the sizzling oil over the curry. Rest 2 minutes with the lid on, then taste and add salt or a squeeze more green chilli.
🌉 US & Bay Area Ingredient Adaptations
  • Fresh Gongura (Roselle / Sorrel leaves): Frozen chopped gongura (available in freezer aisles at Patel Brothers / New India Bazaar in Sunnyvale & Santa Clara). If completely unavailable, use fresh Sorrel leaves (found at local Bay Area Farmers Markets or Whole Foods) or baby spinach whisked with 1.5 tbsp fresh lemon juice / amchur to simulate the acidic tang.
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Patel Brothers (Santa Clara), New India Bazaar (Sunnyvale/Santa Clara), Apni Mandi (Fremont), or local weekend farmers markets.
  • Fresh Tiger Prawns (Royyalu): Frozen peeled, deveined tail-off wild Gulf or Argentine red shrimp (21/25 or 16/20 count from Costco, Trader Joe's, or Safeway). Thaw in cold water for 10 minutes and pat dry with paper towels before searing.
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Costco, Trader Joe's, Safeway, Whole Foods seafood counter.
Make it taste like home
  • Taste tip: red-stemmed gongura is sharper than green-stemmed; with red leaves, start with 100 g, taste, and add the rest.
  • Keto tip: the leaf carries the sourness, so there is nothing to balance with jaggery. Do not add tamarind; 10 g of pulp alone would add over 5 g of carbohydrate.
  • Batch tip: cook the gongura pulp (steps 2, 4 and 5 without the prawns) in a double batch, freeze it in ice-cube trays for up to a month, and make the prawns fresh in 10 minutes.
  • Heat dial: heat 4 as written. For a milder dish, drop the dried chillies and halve the Guntur karam.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • Swap the prawns for 400 g boneless chicken thigh in 2 cm pieces (sear 4 minutes, then simmer 8 minutes in the masala) until the chicken reaches 74 C / 165 F with no pink inside.
  • No fresh gongura? Use thawed frozen chopped gongura in step 5 in place of the fresh leaves, squeeze out the excess water, and cook until the oil separates.

Gasagasala Paneer Kura (Poppy-Coconut Paneer Curry)గసగసాల పనీర్ కూర

Soft paneer in a silky, faintly sweet gravy of toasted poppy seeds, coconut and almonds, lifted with green chilli and curry leaves. It borrows the poppy-seed paste of Andhra gasagasala kura and the coconut of paneer kobbari kura, with no onion, tomato or sugar.

8.0g net carbs  ·  23g protein  ·  41g fat  ·  497 kcal  ·  Serves 2
● Fat 75% ● Protein 19% ● Net Carbs 6%
🎬 Video Walkthrough & Cooking Technique: Watch on YouTube →
DhabaStyle KAJU PANEER MASALA|కాజు పనీర్ మసాలా|ఈ పనీర్ కర్రీ రుచి ఎప్పటికి గుర్తుండిపోతుంది — Vismai Food
Ingredients
  • paneer, cut into 2 cm cubes one 200 g block · 200g
  • fresh or frozen grated coconut 1/3 cup · 30g
  • poppy seeds (gasagasalu) 1 tbsp plus 1 tsp · 10g
  • raw almonds about 7 almonds · 8g
  • ghee 1 tbsp plus 1/2 tsp · 16g
  • green chillies (2 in the paste, rest slit) 3 to 4 chillies · 14g
  • garlic 1 large clove · 5g
  • ginger 1 cm piece · 4g
  • Guntur karam (red chilli powder) 1 tsp · 2.5g
  • coriander powder 3/4 tsp · 2.5g
  • turmeric powder a good pinch · 0.6g
  • cumin seeds 1/3 tsp · 1g
  • mustard seeds 1/3 tsp · 1g
  • curry leaves 1 sprig · 2g
  • cinnamon stick, a 1 cm piece 0.4g
  • green cardamom pods, lightly crushed 2 pods · 0.5g
  • fresh coriander leaves, chopped 2 tbsp · 10g
  • salt about 1/2 tsp · 3g
Method
  1. Dry-roast the poppy seeds in a small pan over low heat for 45 to 60 seconds, shaking constantly, until they smell nutty and look a shade darker but not brown. Tip them into a bowl with the almonds and cover with 1/2 cup of just-boiled water. Soak 10 minutes.
  2. Meanwhile, drop the paneer cubes into a bowl of hot salted water and leave them for 5 minutes; this keeps them soft and well seasoned. Drain just before use.
  3. Drain the poppy seeds and almonds. Blend them with the coconut, 2 of the green chillies, the garlic, the ginger and 150 ml of fresh water for a full 2 minutes, scraping down once, until the paste is completely smooth and creamy.
  4. Heat the ghee in a kadai over medium heat. Add the mustard seeds, cumin, cinnamon and cardamom. When the mustard stops popping (about 20 seconds) add the curry leaves and the remaining slit green chillies and let them splutter for 15 seconds.
  5. Lower the heat. Stir in the Guntur karam, coriander powder and turmeric for 15 seconds only (powders burn in seconds in ghee), then pour in the blended paste plus 100 ml of water and the salt.
  6. Simmer on medium-low for 7 to 8 minutes, stirring every minute because poppy-seed gravies catch on the bottom, until the gravy thickens to a coating consistency and tiny beads of ghee float at the edges.
  7. Add the drained paneer, fold gently and warm through 3 minutes with the lid on. Add a splash of hot water if the gravy tightens. Finish with the coriander leaves and rest 3 minutes before serving; the gravy thickens a little as it stands.
🌉 US & Bay Area Ingredient Adaptations
  • White Poppy Seeds (Gasagasalu): Raw white sesame seeds (nuvvulu) or blanched almond flour ground with warm water. This yields the exact same rich, nutty, low-carb thickening body without altering cooking times.
    Where to buy in the Bay Area: Indian grocers or Mediterranean markets; almond flour is available at Costco, Trader Joe's, or Safeway.
Make it taste like home
  • Taste tip: roasting and then soaking the poppy seeds is what gives this gravy its deep, nutty body. Do not skip the full 2 minutes of blending or the gravy will taste gritty.
  • Keto tip: the gravy is built on seeds and coconut instead of onion, tomato, cashew or sugar. Use a brand of paneer with about 3 g carbohydrate per 100 g or less (check the label).
  • Make-ahead: the blended paste keeps 2 days in the fridge or 1 month frozen in ice-cube trays. Reheat gently with a splash of water; paneer toughens if boiled hard.
  • Heat dial: heat 3 as written. For heat 4, add 1/2 tsp more karam and one more green chilli to the paste; for heat 2, use two chillies in total.
Keto Swaps & Variations
  • Use 300 g boneless chicken thigh pieces instead of paneer and add about 100 ml more water: simmer in the gravy for 12 to 15 minutes until the chicken reaches 74 C / 165 F with no pink inside.
  • Cauliflower florets (150 g, lightly steamed) can replace half the paneer, at roughly 1 g extra net carbohydrate for the pan.

Keto science and tips

MIT Technology Review: today · 2m
MIT report: keto-diet tumour risk in mouse small intestine traced to fat burning, not ketones  —  MIT Technology Review's short piece reports an MIT study in which mice on a ketogenic diet developed more tumours in the small intestine than mice on a control diet, while the same diet suppressed colon tumours. The small-intestine effect was driven by how intestinal cells burn dietary fat (fatty acid oxidation), which activates proteins that make stem cells multiply faster. Ketone bodies such as BHB, once credited with the colon protection, were not involved: 'metabolic bystanders', says researcher Omer Yilmaz, who cautions against generalising the effects of these diets. This is mouse work, not evidence about people.
Takeaway: Your keto-Andhra cooking is fat-heavy; this study separates how the gut handles dietary fat from ketones themselves, a useful caveat when reading keto-science headlines.
BMJ Open (via PubMed Central): Sep 11 · 8m
Meta-analysis: low-carb diet quality, not carb count alone, tracks with mortality in cohort studies  —  A systematic review pooled 18 prospective cohort studies (779,158 participants, 219,457 deaths) that scored low-carbohydrate eating patterns as overall, healthy or unhealthy. In the general population the highest healthy-score group showed no significant mortality difference (HR 0.93). Among people with existing chronic disease it was lower (HR 0.72). Unhealthy scores showed no association. The authors read this as food quality and source mattering more than carbohydrate cut-off. Observational data, not a keto trial.
Takeaway: Fits your keto sustainability reading: it asks whether what replaces the carbs (food quality and source) matters as much as the carb number itself.
Discover Chemistry (Springer Nature): Oct 6 · 6m
Lab study heats cow ghee from 60 °C to a 500 °C furnace set point and tracks what it releases  —  Researchers at Swarnandhra College of Engineering and Technology in Narsapur, Andhra Pradesh, heated 50 g ghee samples in three stages. At 60 to 70 °C free fatty acids rose from 0.42% to 0.71%. Acrolein, an irritating aldehyde, peaked at 112.6 micrograms per gram at 300 °C. At the 500 °C furnace set point an unidentified ion appeared. It is a small, five-replicate lab study, and the authors flag the top temperature as an upper bound.
Takeaway: Ghee is a core fat in your keto-Andhra interest, and this open-access chemistry comes from an Andhra Pradesh college.

It also calls for clean covered vehicles, food-grade packaging instead of gunny bags, protection from dust, moisture and pests, and records that allow consignments to be traced. Non-compliance is described as a breach of the licensing and hygiene regulations.
