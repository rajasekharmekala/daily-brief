### The Ten-Year Yield Climbs Toward Five Percent and Reprices Every Growth Stock

*Investigated & Cross-Examined: FRED: 10-Year Treasury Constant Maturity Rate, US Treasury Daily Par Yield Curve Rates, Trading Economics: US 10-Year Bond Yield*

Here is the quiet story behind every loud headline. The ten-year Treasury yield finished Friday near four point eight six percent. A year ago, that number was closer to four point one. Think of the ten-year yield as the baseline gravitational pull of safe money. When the government pays nearly five percent to do nothing, every risky investment must work harder to earn its keep.

The math is unforgiving, and it is not about earnings at all. It is about discounting. A dollar of profit ten years from now is worth less today when safe rates are higher. Tech stocks bake most of their value into the distant future, so they feel that pull most. That is why the Nasdaq fell harder than the Dow, which is full of today's cash machines.

Now look at the mechanism, because this is where people get lost. When yields rise, bond prices fall. Portfolios that must hold a fixed mix of stocks and bonds then sell stocks to rebalance. That selling is mechanical, not emotional. It is the same plumbing that forced selling in 2022. Today AI capex is debt-financed, so higher yields hit builders twice.

What we make of it is this. We are not in a broken market. We are in a market that finally cares about the price of money again. For your portfolio, that favors businesses that fund themselves from cash flow. Arista and TSMC throw off cash. Levered builders do not. That distinction will separate winners from casualties all winter.

> **THE DISCOUNT RATE IS THE STORY:** Our read is clear. The move in yields is doing more damage than any single earnings miss. We are not calling a crash. We are calling a rotation. Own the tollbooths that generate cash today. Treat the leveraged builders with new respect for their debt. Expect another leg lower before quality names find a floor.

### Inside the Panic: VIX, Options Skew, and the Insurance Price Tag
*Lead Reporting: [Cboe & CNN Markets](https://www.cnn.com/markets/fear-and-greed)*
*Investigated & Cross-Examined: CNN Markets: Fear and Greed Index and put/call ratio, Cboe VIX Volatility Products market data, CNBC: Wall Street's fear gauge is ripping*

Now let us open the options pit, because that is where fear gets a price tag. The VIX, the market's so-called fear gauge, spiked to about twenty-six last week before easing to roughly twenty-one. The VIX measures how much traders expect the S&P to swing over the next month. Above twenty signals nerves. Above thirty signals panic. We touched nerves, not panic.

The more revealing number is the put to call ratio, near one point zero on a five-day average. Puts are bets that prices fall. Calls are bets that prices rise. A ratio near one means traders buy about as much protection as hope. Earlier in the year, when everyone was euphoric, that ratio was much lower. Nobody wanted insurance. Today, many do.

Here is the mechanism that explains why moves get violent. Options skew is simply the market's insurance price tag. When traders panic, they bid up downside put insurance, so protection costs far more than upside bets. The brokers selling those policies must hedge, buying and selling futures to stay balanced. That hedging is mechanical, and it can move markets for reasons that have nothing to do with earnings.

What we make of it is this. Elevated hedging is a two-sided signal. It confirms caution, but it also sets up a squeeze higher if news turns friendly. All that protection must be unwound eventually. For your portfolio, the expensive insurance is a reminder to own quality rather than chase bounce trades. We watch the skew as a weather report, not a forecast.

> **HEDGING IS NOT CAPITULATION:** Our read is that the options market shows caution, not despair. The VIX at twenty-one is elevated but far from a crisis. We are not calling a bottom, because rates still rule the tape. But when hedging is this popular, the downside is partly paid for.

*Investigated & Cross-Examined: Tapeboard: High short interest stocks squeeze leaderboard, Charles Schwab: What is a short squeeze and why does it happen, Business Insider: AI stocks crater as investors panic*

Every stampede has a shape, and in this market it is the short squeeze. A short seller borrows a share, sells it, and hopes to buy it back cheaper. Think of shorts as people crowded into a small room with one narrow exit. When good news breaks, everyone panics and rushes for the door at once. To leave, they must buy shares back, and that buying rockets the price.

The current poster child is IREN, a data center operator tied to AI computing. As of Friday's close, roughly a quarter of its tradeable shares were sold short, according to the squeeze leaderboard. That is extraordinary crowding, well above the single-digit levels of a typical stock. IREN had already swung violently, falling more than fourteen percent in a session.

Here is why this matters for the whole market. When speculative names are heavily shorted, they amplify both directions of the tape. On the way down, selling compounds as funds dump winners to cover losers. On the way up, the rush to cover can produce rallies that look irrational. In 2021, similar crowding turned sleepy stocks into rockets.

What we make of it is this. We do not chase squeezes, because the exit is always narrower than the entrance. For your portfolio, the lesson is that the AI complex is now a crowded trade in both directions. That argues for owning the durable infrastructure names where fundamentals anchor the price. We use squeezes as a warning label, not a shopping list.

> **CROWDED IN BOTH DIRECTIONS:** Our read is that heavy short interest in the neoclouds is a symptom of a fragile trade, not an opportunity. We hold no position in the squeeze candidates. The structural names in our book, like Arista and TSMC, are not squeeze plays. They are businesses.

### The Derivatives Book: LEAPS, Tactical Index Instruments, and Discipline
*Lead Reporting: [Bloomberg & Company Filings](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ai-bubble-financial-markets/)*
*Investigated & Cross-Examined: Bloomberg: AI has taken over every corner of the market, Fidelity: Signs of an AI bubble to watch, Yahoo Finance: Oracle debt and AI funding plan*

Finally, let us look at how we express conviction through derivatives, because this is where discipline matters most. We hold long-dated call options, called LEAPS, on several high-conviction compounders. A call option is the right, not the obligation, to buy a stock at a set price before a set date. Think of it as a restaurant reservation you may not use. You pay a small deposit for the chance to buy later.

The appeal of long-dated options is leverage with a long runway. We hold these on companies like Amazon, Adobe, SoFi, Walmart, and Uber. That is our bet on steady compounders rather than speculative moonshots. We also use tactical index instruments, which move two or three times the daily move of an index. They are tools for short-term exposure, not buy-and-hold vehicles.

Here is the catch with leverage, in plain language. Options and leveraged funds amplify both directions. When volatility rises, the insurance embedded in those prices gets more expensive, so the same bet costs more. Last week's spike in fear made every derivative position pricier to hold.

What we make of it is this. Derivatives are the accelerator and the brake of a portfolio, never the engine. The long-dated calls on steady businesses are a reasonable way to hold upside without risking the core. The tactical index funds are for conviction over days and weeks, not years. We keep the core in real businesses and use the derivatives for edge.

> **LEVERAGE IS A TOOL, NOT A TEMPERAMENT:** Our read is that the derivatives book should stay modest in a high-volatility, high-rate regime. We keep the long-dated calls on steady compounders as an upside reserve. We keep the tactical index instruments small and short-dated. And we hold the core in cash-generating businesses like Arista and TSMC.

### Did India's Derivatives Crackdown Actually Save Retail Traders? Read the Study
*Lead Reporting: [SEBI Profitability Study FY25–FY26 & Press Release 50/2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787236407005.pdf)*
*Investigated & Cross-Examined: SEBI PR No.50/2026, Retail Derivatives Studies, SEBI Profitability of Individual Traders in EDS (FY25–FY26), The Hindu BusinessLine on SEBI's FY26 Findings, India Today: Did SEBI's Measures Reduce Trader Losses?*

> **FEWER PLAYERS, SAME BROKEN GAME:** SEBI deserves credit for honest measurement and for cooling the most frenzied index-options activity. But the evidence in its own study is that the core problem survives. Retail options trading remains a wealth-transfer machine from the impatient many to the patient few. The lesson for our listener is blunt. Avoid single-stock and weekly index options as a wealth strategy. If you trade them, treat it as entertainment money you can lose in full, and size it that way. ## DESK V • AI & AGENT ENGINEERING + BUILDER'S PLAYBOOK: The Harness Is the Product: How the Software Around the Model Won 2026

> *"The model is the engine. The harness is the chassis and dashboard around it, and in 2026 that is where the real engineering battles are fought."* — **AI & Agent Engineering Desk**

Good morning, and welcome to the builder's desk, where we stop admiring the engine and examine the car.

For three years the whole conversation was about the model itself. How many parameters, how much data, how clever the reasoning. That still matters. But the most interesting work of 2026 is happening in the software wrapped around the model. Engineers call it the harness.

Think of it this way. The model is the engine under the hood. A bare engine on a workbench is powerful but useless. It cannot steer or brake. The harness is the chassis and dashboard that turn raw horsepower into a car you can drive. Same engine, wildly different cars.

Today we unpack four moving pieces. We will look at frontier models built to be driven. We will open an open-source harness where every part is swappable. We will audit the evals that supposedly prove these agents work. And we will hand you a production lesson for your own systems.

### Frontier Models Now Ship Built for Agents, Not Chat
*Lead Reporting: [Meta Newsroom & METR Time Horizons](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/10/introducing-muse-wonder-models/)*
*Investigated & Cross-Examined: Meta Newsroom: Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, METR Task-Completion Time Horizons of Frontier AI Models, Parallel: What is an agent harness?*

For years, new model releases bragged about the same thing. Better scores on trivia, better essays. That race is maturing. The new race is about who can finish a real job, over hours, without falling apart.

Meta made this pivot explicit in October. Their new models carry an Active Memory, which holds context across separate conversations. They also ship an Active Focus, which keeps pushing toward a goal when a task gets hard. Think of Active Focus as a dog with a bone. It tries new routes and recovers from errors.

This matters because the measuring stick changed too. A nonprofit called METR tracks the task-completion time horizon. In plain English, that is the length of a real job, in human hours, that an agent can finish about half the time. Think of it like a runner's longest reliable distance. A five-hour score is not every five-hour job. It is roughly half of them.

So what changed under the hood? The models stopped being single answers and became workers in a loop. They plan, act, observe the result, and try again. Each turn, a smart harness keeps a small index card of working notes beside the keyboard. That card, called the KV cache, spares the model from re-reading a five-hundred-page manual every turn. The trick of 2026 is keeping that card accurate for hours.

> **LONG HORIZON IS THE REAL SCOREBOARD:** We think the shift from chat quality to agent endurance is the most important engineering story of the year. It rewards labs that design for memory, recovery, and follow-through. For builders, the lesson is blunt. Stop picking models on benchmark bragging alone. Test them on your own messy, multi-hour jobs. The winner is the one that keeps its working notes straight for the longest stretch.

### DeepSeek Open-Sources a Harness Where Everything Is a Plugin
*Lead Reporting: [DeepSeek Harness GitHub & The New Stack](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/deepseek-harness)*
*Investigated & Cross-Examined: DeepSeek Harness official repository, The New Stack: DeepSeek open sources an agent harness where everything is a plugin*

In August, a Chinese lab handed builders a gift and a challenge at once. DeepSeek open-sourced its agent harness under a permissive MIT license. The repo picked up tens of thousands of stars within hours. On paper, it is another coding agent. In practice, it is a statement about how agent software should be built.

The design philosophy is captured in one line. Everything is a plugin. The part that talks to the model, the registry of tools, the session log, and the loop that drives the agent are all replaceable. There is, as the documentation puts it, no privileged core to patch. Think of a kitchen where every appliance sits on the same universal socket.

The harness keeps a strict append-only log. In everyday terms, this is a court stenographer who records every word that reaches the model. Resuming a task, forking it, or replaying it later all read from that one transcript. The safety story is equally disciplined. On a Mac it wraps each command in a sandbox called Seatbelt. On Linux it uses one called Landlock. Think of these as a child-proof playpen. The agent works freely inside, but cannot wander into the rest of the house.

Here is the sharpest detail. The harness is not tied to DeepSeek's own models. Its catalog spans Anthropic, OpenAI, Amazon, Microsoft and Google. That is a deliberate bet that the harness outlives any single model. When a better engine arrives, you keep the car and swap the motor.

> **THE HARNESS OUTLIVES THE MODEL:** We see this as the clearest signal yet that harnesses are becoming the durable asset. Models improve every few months. A harness with clean tool boundaries, a reliable log, and real sandboxing can absorb every one of those waves. Invest your hours in the parts that stay, and treat the model as a replaceable cartridge.

### The Eval Audit: Automatic Harness Tuning Is Mostly an Illusion
*Lead Reporting: [arXiv: Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12227v2)*
*Investigated & Cross-Examined: arXiv 2607.12227v2: Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution for Agents, Terminal-Bench 2.1 (Laude Institute & Stanford), arXiv 2601.11868: Terminal-Bench benchmarking agents on hard terminal tasks*

Now for the part that should make every builder sit up. A team from the Allen Institute for AI and the University of Washington published a sharp paper in August. It pokes a hole in a fashionable idea. The idea is that an agent can improve its own harness. It sounds like magic. The paper says the magic is mostly an accounting trick.

First, fix the term. An eval is simply a driving test for a model. It is a set of tasks with a clear pass or fail, so you can compare agents fairly. The researchers took a popular coding benchmark called Terminal-Bench and tested a rival method. Instead of tuning the harness, that rival simply made several attempts and picked the best one.

The result was uncomfortable for the hype. Automatic harness tuning did not reliably beat the simple approach of trying more times. Here is why that matters. If you have extra money, you can polish the car, or you can take more practice laps. The paper found that more laps usually won. Worse, the tuning gains mostly evaporated on fresh tasks. The harness had memorized old fixes rather than learning a general skill. Think of a student who memorizes last year's answers. Brilliant on that paper, useless on a new one.

The authors are careful. They note the benchmark may simply be too easy for harness design to shine. On hard enough tasks, a better harness genuinely could expand what an agent can do. But the burden of proof just got heavier. Any vendor claiming a self-improving harness must show it works on tasks it has never seen.

> **DEMAND HELD-OUT TESTS:** We take three things from this audit. First, be skeptical whenever an agent claims to improve itself. Second, hold out a slice of your own tasks, hide them from any tuning loop, and grade only on those. Third, do not confuse extra attempts with a better design, because they cost different money. The practical winner is a solid harness plus a few honest retries.

### The Playbook: What a Production Harness Looks Like in Practice
*Lead Reporting: [Anthropic Engineering: Effective Harnesses & Harness Design](https://www.anthropic.com/research/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)*
*Investigated & Cross-Examined: Anthropic: Effective harnesses for long-running agents, Anthropic: Harness design for long-running application development, Parallel: What is an agent harness?*

So if the harness is where the work lives, what does a good one look like? The most useful evidence comes from Anthropic, which has published two reports on running agents for hours. The findings are refreshingly unglamorous.

The first problem was short-term amnesia. A long job runs across many separate context windows. Think of context windows as the size of an executive's desk. Only so much fits at once. When it fills, the work gets shoved into a drawer. Their fix was an initializer agent that sets up the project and writes a plain progress file. Later sessions read that file, like a mechanic reading the clipboard left by the previous shift.

Their second problem was self-flattery. An agent asked to grade its own work tends to praise it, even when a human sees it is mediocre. The fix was elegant. Separate the doer from the judge. One agent builds, a different agent grades, and the judge is tuned to be skeptical. Think of a restaurant where the chef never reviews their own kitchen. A separate inspector walks in cold, and the food gets better.

Their later work added a planner. It turns a one-line request into a full specification, then a builder works through it feature by feature. A single-agent run took twenty minutes and nine dollars. The full harness took six hours and about two hundred dollars. But the cheap run produced an app whose core feature was broken. The expensive run produced one that worked.

> **FIVE MOVES THAT ACTUALLY SHIP:** Here is the production pattern we would hand to any team this week. One, keep a written progress file and read it at the start of every session. Two, maintain a graded checklist of features, and never let the agent edit that list. Three, split the builder from the grader, and tune the grader to be strict. Four, test end to end through the real interface, not just unit checks. Five, after each new model, remove the scaffolding it no longer needs.

> **ENGINEER THE WORK, NOT JUST THE MODEL:** Our verdict on the whole desk is this. The teams that win the next two years will not have the single best model. They will treat the harness as a first-class product. That means memory that survives a long job, a clean seam for swapping engines, a strict separated grader, and honest evals on unseen work. The model is rented. The harness is owned.

*And with the builder's desk packed up, we turn to a single structural story that ties all of this together. The machines are getting smarter and cheaper to run. But they need something the software world never had to think about before. They need enormous amounts of electricity. That is our Deep Dive Documentary.*

## DESK VI • THE DEEP DIVE DOCUMENTARY: The Watt Bottleneck: How Electricity Became the Real Limit on Artificial Intelligence

> *"The software world spent its whole history believing that its only limits were imagination and electricity bills. Then artificial intelligence arrived and made the second one matter more than the first."* — **Documentary & Structural Research Desk**

Good morning. Clear the desk, because today we are telling one story, from the beginning, all the way to the far horizon.

For two decades, the internet was a business of ideas. Build a clever website, and the servers would follow. Electricity was a line item, not a strategy. It was like the plumbing in a house. You never thought about it until it broke.

Then came artificial intelligence, and the plumbing became the whole house. Today a single rack of AI servers, no bigger than your refrigerator, can draw as much power as dozens of homes. The software industry, which once moved at the speed of a git commit, now waits in line for something far more stubborn. It waits for watts.

This is the story of the year that the artificial intelligence boom collided with the physical world. It is a story about grid queues, gas turbines, batteries, and a quiet shift in the economics of computing. And it will tell you more about where your portfolio is heading than any earnings call this season. Let us begin.

### The Watt Bottleneck: Why Power, Not Chips, Is Now the Real Limit on AI
*Lead Reporting: [IEA Key Questions on Energy and AI & Deloitte TMT Predictions 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary)*
*Investigated & Cross-Examined: IEA: Key Questions on Energy and AI (Executive Summary, 2026), IEA: Data Centres electricity consumption, Deloitte: Why AI's next phase will likely demand more computational power, not less, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory: Queued Up (interconnection queues)*

Let us start with the backstory, because none of this is new. Engineers have warned about a power wall for years. In the 1990s, chip designers hit a ceiling when processors grew too hot to cool. They fixed it by adding cores instead of raw speed. It was like a factory that stopped hiring one superhuman worker and started hiring a hundred ordinary ones. That trick bought two more decades. It just delayed the reckoning.

The modern reckoning arrived with artificial intelligence, and it is measured in something called power density. That is simply how much electricity you pack into a given space. A normal data center rack from a few years ago drew maybe ten to thirty kilowatts. The current generation of AI racks draws well over a hundred. The IEA notes that between 2020 and 2025, AI server power density rose about eleven times. It is set to rise another four times by 2027. Picture a kitchen where you keep swapping in ever-larger stoves, but never widen the counter.

Now the demand picture. The IEA reports that data center electricity use grew seventeen percent in 2025, with the AI-focused slice surging fifty percent. It expects data center consumption to roughly double to about nine hundred and fifty terawatt-hours by 2030. That is around three percent of all the world's electricity. Meanwhile the largest technology companies spent over four hundred billion dollars on capital projects in 2025. The IEA expects that to jump another seventy-five percent in 2026. It made a striking observation. The capital spending of just five tech companies now exceeds global investment in oil and gas production.

This leads to the first great bottleneck, the one nobody outside the energy world talks about. It is the grid connection queue. Before a data center can draw power, it must get permission and a physical hookup from the grid operator. That line is brutally long. The IEA reports that American data center developers are pushing to build their own on-site gas plants. They do this precisely because grid connections are so slow. It notes that supplying reliable on-site gas power requires overbuilding generation capacity by thirty to seventy percent, because the load swings so violently. And gas turbines themselves are now scarce, with orders surging seventy percent in 2025.

Here is the second shift, and it is the one that quietly rewrites the economics of the entire industry. For years, artificial intelligence was mostly about training. Training is teaching a model by feeding it mountains of data, like a student cramming for a final exam. Now the industry has moved to inference, which is simply using the trained model to answer real questions every day. Think of training as building the car and inference as all the driving it will ever do. Deloitte expects inference to reach roughly two-thirds of all computing this year, up from about a third in 2023.

You might think the money follows the everyday driving and not the one-time building. That is only half true. Deloitte makes a subtler and more important point. New techniques called post-training and test-time scaling are extremely power-hungry. Post-training, which refines a model after its first big training run, can use many times the compute of the original. Test-time scaling, the long deliberation a model does before answering a hard question, can use over a hundred times the energy of a simple summary. So the shift to inference does not reduce the power appetite. It multiplies it in a new shape.

Now the physical mechanisms, because this is where the story gets fascinating. The heat problem is the loudest. The IEA notes a single AI rack must evacuate heat equal to about thirty natural gas boilers. You cannot cool that with ordinary fans. That is why liquid cooling, which circulates fluid right past the hot chips like a car's radiator, has moved from novelty to necessity. This is exactly why companies that make liquid cooling systems, like our holding in Vertiv, have become infrastructure plays rather than side bets.

The second mechanism is the electricity grid itself, and its strange new physics. AI loads swing violently, the IEA says, with changes of more than fifty percent of rated capacity within one second. That is like a city where everyone flips their stove on and off together, every second, all day. Traditional power plants and grids hate this. The answer is storage, meaning big batteries that soak up the spikes and smooth them out. The IEA projects twenty to twenty-five gigawatts of battery storage installed inside data centers globally by 2030. Notably, that could turn data centers into a stabilizer for the grid rather than a burden on it.

And then there is the third mechanism, the one that ties directly to the semiconductor theme running through our portfolio. The IEA flags a shortage of high-bandwidth memory, the specialized memory glued right next to the AI chips. Think of it as the chip's scratch pad, and it is the bottleneck inside the bottleneck. The agency expects this shortage to persist through at least the end of 2027. It also warns that advanced power electronics and transformers depend on critical inputs from a small number of producers, notably China. In other words, the constraint has spread from the chip to the sub-components that feed the chip.

So let us draw out the second-order consequences, because this is where your portfolio lives. The first consequence is a change in who holds the power. When electricity becomes the scarce input, the companies that generate and move power gain leverage. That is why our grid cables, transformers, and power generation in India matter. It is also why clean power and fuel cells in the United States are no longer quiet diversifiers. They are direct participants in the artificial intelligence buildout.

The second consequence is that financing becomes the real test. The IEA is explicit that data center investments have grown too large to fund from company balance sheets alone. That means capital markets now decide the pace. If sentiment about returns on artificial intelligence sours, the buildout slows, and every supplier along the chain feels it. This is not a reason to panic, but it is a reason to watch credit conditions as closely as you watch chip announcements.

The third consequence is social, and it is gaining force fast. The IEA reports a rising backlash, as communities push back on data centers over electricity prices and environmental impact. This is the same tension we have seen in India and Europe with large industrial projects. A data center is a very visible neighbor, and visible neighbors attract scrutiny. Expect permitting to become a genuine battlefield in the years ahead, in the United States and abroad.

> **TRAINING VERSUS INFERENCE, IN PLAIN WORDS:** Training is the one-time marathon. You feed a model enormous amounts of data and it learns patterns over weeks. Inference is the everyday driving. Every question you ask, every agent step taken, is a short trip. Here is the twist. The everyday driving is now getting heavier, because models pause to think longer before they answer. That long thinking uses roughly a hundred times the energy of a quick reply.

> **THE RAILROAD AND THE TELEGRAPH:** History offers a clean mirror. In the nineteenth century, the telegraph promised to shrink the world. But it could not lay a single wire without the railroads, which already owned the land, labor, and logistics. The clever new technology rode on the back of heavy old infrastructure. Artificial intelligence is doing the same today with the electric grid, gas turbines, and liquid cooling. The lesson from history is consistent. When a new technology depends on old infrastructure, the old infrastructure captures a large share of the value, at least for a while.

> **POWER IS THE NEW TOLLBOOTH:** Our verdict is that the artificial intelligence boom has quietly changed its physical center of gravity. The winning question is no longer only who has the best model. It is who can get the most power to the most chips, fastest, and cool them without melting. That favors three groups. The power-equipment makers, the cooling specialists, and the firms that can generate or store electricity on site. It penalizes anyone who assumed computing grows without needing land, wires, and laws. This is why we hold clean baseload power, fuel cells, transformers, and liquid cooling across the book. The risk is real, especially if financing tightens or communities block projects. But the direction is clear. In the next ten years, the tollbooth on intelligence will be measured in watts.

*That is the deep dive, and it leaves us with one sobering thought for the week ahead. Every dollar spent on a clever new model is now matched by a dollar spent on the unglamorous work of moving electrons. Keep the grid, the turbines and the transformers in mind. They are quietly setting the ceiling on how fast all of this can grow. From the documentary desk, we now hand you back to the world desk for the policy and geopolitics shaping that very buildout.*

### The Supreme Court Killed the Emergency Tariffs, and Now the Refund Queue Begins
*Lead Reporting: [Congressional Research Service & Holland & Knight](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R49481)*
*Investigated & Cross-Examined: CRS Fact Sheet: Active Presidential Tariff Actions in 2026, Holland & Knight: Court of International Trade Orders Nationwide Tariff Refunds, Thompson Coburn Trade Alert: Supreme Court Issues Ruling on IEEPA Tariffs*

Here is a story about getting your money back, slowly. For a year, importers paid extra duties at the border under an emergency law.

On February 20, the Supreme Court ruled six to three that the law did not authorize those tariffs. The toll booth was, in effect, illegal.

But the Court said nothing about refunds. It left that mess to the lower courts. Hundreds of businesses had already sued just to preserve a claim.

Then on March 4, the Court of International Trade ordered refunds for all importers. Not only the ones who had sued. Everyone who paid.

Now the plumbing gets interesting. To recover the money, importers file protests with Customs, or ask the trade court to reliquidate entries.

Roughly a thousand businesses had already sought refunds before the ruling. That number has kept climbing since.

The dissenting justices warned this would be a mess. Justice Kavanaugh called it exactly that. He was right about the arithmetic.

> **REFUNDS ARE REAL BUT SLOW:** The refund will happen, and it is genuinely owed. But the mechanism runs through paperwork, interest calculations, and years of possible appeals. For our clean-energy and industrial holdings, this is background noise, not a windfall. Where it matters is uncertainty. When tariff rules keep shifting, companies delay capital spending. That hesitation, more than the tariff itself, is the real cost to the economy. Watch for the alternate authorities the administration is using now, because those are the toll booths still standing.

### Bay Area Voters Reopen the Transit Tills, and the Real Bill Comes Later
*Lead Reporting: [Metropolitan Transportation Commission & SFCTA](https://mtc.ca.gov/news/bay-area-voters-show-strong-support-public-transit)*
*Investigated & Cross-Examined: MTC: Bay Area Voters Show Strong Support for Public Transit, SFCTA: Bay Area Voters Approve Transit Funding Measures, SF Standard: The Bay Area has decided public transit is worth saving*

For two years, Bay Area transit chiefs warned of a cliff. Ridership had not fully returned, and federal relief had run dry.

On November 3, voters answered. The Regional Transit Measure passed with about sixty-one and a half percent support across five counties.

Think of a transit system like a household with a mortgage and no raise. The tax is a second income, not a windfall.

The measure adds a half-cent sales tax in four counties and a full cent in San Francisco, for fourteen years. It raises about $980 million a year.

San Francisco voters also passed Proposition H, a parcel tax to steady Muni. That one cleared with about seventy-two percent of the vote.

So the trains keep running. BART, Muni, Caltrain, and AC Transit stave off deep service cuts they had publicly threatened.

But here is the catch, and it is the honest one. BART still faces a deficit of about fifty-seven million dollars even with its share.

Muni still carries a structural shortfall. The tax buys time, not solvency. The money simply expires in fourteen years.

> **TIME BOUGHT, NOT A FIX:** This is a rational decision by voters who value the network. Transit keeps the region's labor market functional, and a collapse would have hurt every business near a station. But note the shape of what passed. It is a sunset tax with oversight reviews attached, which tells you the agencies did not trust themselves to reform fast enough without pressure. The second-order risk is moral hazard: guaranteed money now can delay the hard work of cutting duplicate costs across four agencies.
